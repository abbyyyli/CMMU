    1. Leadership and management

    The lecture calls them “distinct, yet complementary.” They are two different jobs.

    Management

    Controls complexity. Keeps things running with plans, budgets and checks.

    Leadership

    Produces useful change. Gives people a direction and gets them to commit to it.

    Healthy organizations need both.

    2. Trait theory

    Traits are personal qualities that set leaders apart from followers. This theory asks what kind of person becomes a leader.

    TraitIn plain words
    Intelligence (EQ and IQ)Thinking ability and emotional skill
    Dominance or assertivenessWilling to take charge and speak up
    Self-confidenceTrusts their own judgment
    Energy and activityHas drive and stamina
    Task-relevant knowledgeKnows the work they lead

    Traits describe the leader. They do not say what to do in a given situation. The next theories fill that gap.

    連到前面S5 的 EQ 在這裡是領導特質之一。

    3. Behavioral theories

    The Ohio State studies looked at what leaders do. They found two kinds of behavior.

    BehaviorWhat it isAsk yourselfDo not confuse with
    Consideration 關懷Building mutual trust, respect and relationshipsDoes the leader build trust?Lowering standards to keep people happy
    Initiating structure 定結構Setting goals, defining tasks and checking the workDoes the leader organize the work?Watching every step all day

    Leaders tend to lean toward one. Effective leadership combines both.

    中文邏輯關懷看「人」，定結構看「事」。到情境領導時，這兩個就是 support 和 task。

    4. Situational leadership

    There is no single best style. The right style depends on the situation, and above all on the follower.

    What shapes the situation

    AreaFactors the lecture lists
    PeopleThe leader, the followers, the leader’s boss, key colleagues
    OrganizationPower of the role, company policies, norms and traditions (culture), constraints
    The workJob demands, the nature of the task, time to decide

    Readiness

    Readiness = ability + willingness, for one specific task.

    Ability 能力

    Can they do it? Look at skill, knowledge, experience with the task, past results and deadlines met.

    Willingness 意願

    Do they want to, and do they feel sure? Look at confidence, commitment, interest, initiative and persistence.

    The same person can be R4 on one task and R1 on a new one.

    Four levels, four styles

    FollowerStyleWhat the leader does
    R1: unable, and unwilling or insecureS1 Directing 指導Says what to do, how and when. Checks that they understand. Gives regular feedback. Talk is mostly one-way.
    R2: unable, but willing or confidentS2 Coaching 教練Still teaches the task and sets deadlines. Also explains why it matters, shows confidence in them and solves some problems together.
    R3: able, but unwilling or insecureS3 Supporting 支持Outlines the task only. Talks through how and when with them. Solves problems together. Gives credit.
    R4: able, and willing or confidentS4 Delegating 授權Agrees on goals and a follow-up plan. Steps in only when they cannot solve a problem. Gives credit.

    Task behavior follows ability: the less able, the more direction. Support is highest in the middle, for R2 and R3.

    中文邏輯能力決定要不要教，意願決定要不要鼓勵。

    Pairs people mix up

    PairHow to tell them apart
    R2 / R3Both need high support. R2 cannot do the task yet, so the leader still teaches. R3 already can, so the leader stops teaching and encourages instead.
    S4 / leaving people aloneS4 still sets goals, follows up and gives credit. Leaving people alone with no follow-up is laissez-faire.

    How to answer a situational leadership question

    1. Name the task.
    2. Judge ability with evidence.
    3. Judge willingness with evidence.
    4. Name the R level and the matching style.
    5. Say what the leader should do.
    6. Say what comes next. As readiness rises, move one step toward S4. If it drops, move one step back.

    5. Transactional leadership

    Leadership as a trade: “what I give you in return for what you’ll do for me.” The leader directs and controls to reach the organization’s goals.

    FormWhat it looks likeExample
    Contingent reward 依績效給獎勵Rewards are tied to performanceA bonus for hitting the monthly target
    Management by exception, activeWatches closely and tracks mistakesChecking every report for errors
    Management by exception, passiveWaits until a problem is reported, then steps inActing only after a customer complains

    6. Transformational leadership

    Leaders who grow their people, help them see old problems in new ways, and inspire them to give more than expected.

    DimensionWhat it isExampleDo not confuse with
    Inspirational motivation 激勵鼓舞Gives people an attractive vision of the future“We make the parts no one else can make.”A sales target with no larger purpose
    Idealized influence 理想化影響Acts the way they want others to act, and earns respectFollows the safety rules even when it costs timeBeing famous or popular
    Intellectual stimulation 智力啟發Gets people to rethink problems and build their own skills“How would you fix this old delivery problem?”Asking for ideas, then ignoring them
    Individualized consideration 個別關懷Coaches each person based on their own needsA monthly growth talk with each personOhio State consideration, which builds trust with the whole group

    The lecture groups the first two under “charisma.” Together the four are often called the four I’s.

    TransactionalTransformational
    The dealWork in exchange for rewardsGrowth in exchange for commitment
    EffortWhat was agreedMore than expected, often more than they thought possible
    Main toolsRewards and monitoringVision, role modeling, new ideas, coaching

    Long-term success may need both.

    7. The full-range leadership model

    All the styles on one chart. The more active the leader, and the more they develop people, the more effective they are.

    Laissez-faire means avoiding decisions and not leading at all. The slides show it but do not define it. The chart has one box for management by exception, covering both the active and passive forms.

    8. What makes a vision work

    Kantabutra’s research: people commit only when they know where the organization is going. A clear vision also gets different groups pulling in the same direction.

    AttributeIn plain wordsAsk yourself
    ConcisenessShortCan people remember it?
    ClarityEasy to understandDoes everyone read it the same way?
    Future orientationAbout the long-term futureDoes it say where we want to be?
    StabilityDoes not change oftenWill it still hold in five years?
    ChallengeHard to reachDoes it make people stretch?
    AbstractnessA broad direction, not one targetIs it bigger than one number?
    Ability to inspireMakes people want to work for itDoes it give people a reason to care?

    Source: Kantabutra and Avery (2010).

    Weak

    “Increase sales by 8% next year.” A one-year number. It is not about the long-term future, not a broad direction, and not inspiring.

    Stronger

    “Make the parts no one else can make.” Short, clear, long-term, hard to reach and broad. The 8% can sit under it as a goal.

    9. Precision Plastics: Session 9 task

    Team activity: situational leadership

    Find the R level of the tool and die workers and of the molding machine workers. Find the S levels of Joe and Bill. Recommend the leadership style and actions that would improve the situation. Present to class.

    Group activity: transformational leadership (30 minutes)

    Use the transformational framework to say what is wrong with the current leadership style, and recommend improvements. Present to class.

    The second activity does not name a case. This page applies it to Precision Plastics.

    The case in brief

    WhoWhat the case says
    The companyMakes small, precise plastic parts for medical and photo equipment. Its molds are hard to design, so it has few competitors. Known for quality.
    Tool and die workersMake the molds. Every mold is different. Highly skilled, experienced, proud of their work and well paid.
    Joe CaseyRan tool and die. Focused on quality and let workers decide how to do their work.
    Molding machine workersRoutine, low-skill, low-pay work. They ignore safety rules and turnover is high. Bill says they have little interest in the job.
    Bill SmithRuns molding. Believes in close supervision and enjoys it. His department is productive and on schedule.
    What changedJoe retires. Bill takes over tool and die too. He adds rules, pushes for speed, tries to make everyone work the same way, and tells experts how to work though he knows little about the craft.
    The resultWorkers complain. Work falls behind. One long-time worker quits. Workers tell Jim Hunt, the production manager, that Bill is a poor supervisor.

    Diagnosis: R levels and S styles

    WhoEvidenceVerdict
    Tool and die workersSkilled, experienced, proud, high qualityR4 → R3 R4 under Joe. Under Bill their skill is the same, but their willingness has dropped.
    Molding machine workersIgnore safety rules, high turnover, low interest (Bill’s view)R1 On following safety rules. High turnover means many are new. A few experienced workers may be R3.
    Joe CaseyQuality focus, lets workers decideS4: fits Matches R4 workers. Result: high quality and happy workers.
    Bill SmithClose supervision, new rules, tells people how to workS1: fits molding only Works for R1 molding workers. Wrong for R4 tool and die workers.

    The core problem: Bill used S1 on R4 experts. They needed S4. Directing able people tells them they are not trusted.

    Other theoryWhat it adds
    TraitBill lacks task-relevant knowledge of tool and die work.
    BehavioralHigh on structure, low on consideration.
    SituationHe ignored the nature of the task (custom, skilled work) and the existing culture (informal, autonomous).

    Transformational view of Bill

    DimensionWhat Bill doesWhat is missing
    Idealized influenceTells experts how to work without knowing the craftRespect. Workers do not see him as a role model.
    Inspirational motivationTalks only about speedA vision. The company wins on quality, and he never mentions it.
    Intellectual stimulationPicks one method himself and imposes itUsing the workers’ expertise to solve problems
    Individualized considerationControls everyone the same wayAttention to each worker’s skill and pride

    Bill is transactional, mainly management by exception, active: he checks constantly and catches problems. That sits near the bottom of the full-range model. For routine molding work, clear rules plus rewards still make sense.

    What we know, infer and cannot conclude

    KnowInferCannot conclude
    Complaints started after Bill arrivedHis style caused the drop in willingnessThat the workers are unwilling by nature
    One long-time worker quitMore may leave if nothing changesHow many will leave
    Molding workers ignore safety rulesMany are new to the jobThat they do not care. Only Bill says so.
    Issue statement

    Precision Plastics put a directing, transactional supervisor in charge of R4 experts. His close control works for routine molding, but it tells skilled tool and die workers they are not trusted. Their willingness is falling, work is late and one expert has quit. That puts the company’s core skill at risk. The molding department still has a safety and turnover problem that close control alone has not fixed.

    10. Worked recommendations

    Read the task→Judge R levels→Match S styles→Find the mismatch→Act, explain, check

    Owner: Jim Hunt, the production manager. He assigned the supervisors and has heard the complaints.

    Tool and die department

    ActionWhy it worksTiming and check
    1. Change the leader. Make a respected senior tool and die maker the lead, or hire an experienced supervisor. Bill goes back to molding.Bill enjoys close control, so he is unlikely to change fast. The new lead knows the craft.Within a month. Check: the lead is in place.
    2. Start with S3, then go back to S4. The new lead listens to complaints, drops rules that do not help quality, and agrees goals and deadlines with the workers.The workers are able but unhappy, so they are R3 for now. Once trust returns, delegate again.First three months. Check: work back on schedule, complaints stop.
    3. Let the workers lead any standardization. They compare methods, choose what to share, and keep room for custom work.Every mold is different. The experts know which steps can be shared.Three to six months. Check: quality stays high, speed improves.
    4. Hold stay interviews, including with the worker who left.Keeps rare skills and shows the company is listening.Within a month. Check: no more quits.
    5. Start an apprentice program where senior workers train young hires.The workers already worry that young people are not entering the trade. This gives them a purpose and keeps the skill alive.Plan within six months. Check: apprentices hired and staying.

    Molding department

    ActionWhy it worksTiming and check
    6. Keep S1 for safety, then move toward S2. Bill keeps clear rules and checks, but also explains why each rule matters and praises safe work.The workers are R1 on safety. As they improve, add explanation and support.Monthly. Check: safety violations and accidents.
    7. Reward safety and attendance.Rewards for results work better than only catching mistakes, and suit routine jobs.Quarterly. Check: turnover and violations.
    8. Coach Bill to read each group’s readiness.He uses one style for everyone.Three months. Check: Bill can explain each group’s R level and his plan.

    For the whole plant, Jim and the new lead share one simple vision about quality, such as “Make the parts no one else can make.” It gives both departments a better reason than “faster.”

    Risks

    There may be no suitable internal lead, and an outside hire needs time to earn trust. Bill may feel demoted, so present the move as using his strength. Delegating fully again before trust is rebuilt.

    What to track

    Tool and die: on-time delivery, quality and rework, quits. Molding: safety violations, accidents, turnover and output.

    60 to 90 second presentation answer
    “The tool and die workers are skilled, experienced and proud of their work. Under Joe they were R4, and Joe used S4, delegating. He gave them freedom and focused on quality, so it worked. The molding workers do routine work, ignore safety rules and leave often. They are R1, so Bill’s S1 directing style fits them, and their output is high. The trouble started when Bill took over tool and die. He used S1 on R4 experts. He checked them constantly and told them how to work, though he knew little about it. Their skill did not change, but their willingness dropped. Work fell behind and one long-time worker quit. We recommend that Jim Hunt move Bill back to molding and make a respected senior tool and die maker the lead. The new lead starts with S3, listening and solving problems together, then goes back to S4 once trust returns. Any standardization should come from the workers. In molding, Bill keeps clear safety rules but moves toward S2, explaining the rules and rewarding safe work. We would track delivery, quality, quits and safety incidents.”

    Questions the professor may ask

    Why not just train Bill to delegate?

    He enjoys close control and does not know the craft. Training takes time, and skilled workers are already leaving. A new lead is faster. Coaching Bill still helps in molding.

    Are the molding workers R1 or R3?

    Mostly R1 on safety. Their willingness is low, and high turnover means many are new. A few experienced ones may be R3.

    Is standardization wrong?

    Not in itself. Forcing it without knowing the craft is the problem. The workers should lead it.

    11. Do not mix these up

    Leadership is better than management.

    They are different jobs. Organizations need both.

    Traits tell you what a leader should do.

    Traits describe the person. Situational theory says what to do.

    R1 followers need lots of support.

    They first need clear direction. Support rises at R2.

    Transactional leadership is bad.

    It works for routine tasks. Long-term success may need both types.

    An abstract vision is a vague one.

    Abstract means a broad direction. It should still be clear.

    Bill is a bad leader.

    His style fits one group and not the other. The problem is the match.

    The molding workers are lazy.

    Only Bill says they lack interest. Use the evidence: safety violations and turnover.

    12. Cold call practice

    Leadership versus management?

    Management controls complexity. Leadership produces useful change. Organizations need both.

    What is trait theory?

    Leaders differ from followers in traits such as intelligence, confidence and task knowledge.

    What are the two Ohio State behaviors?

    Consideration builds trust. Initiating structure organizes the work. Effective leaders combine both.

    What is readiness?

    A follower’s ability and willingness to do one specific task.

    Match R1 to R4 with the styles.

    R1 Directing, R2 Coaching, R3 Supporting, R4 Delegating.

    What is transactional leadership?

    Leading through exchange: rewards for performance, and stepping in when things go wrong.

    Name the four I’s.

    Idealized influence, inspirational motivation, intellectual stimulation, individualized consideration.

    What does the full-range model show?

    Styles from least to most effective: laissez-faire at the bottom, the four I’s at the top.

    Name the seven vision attributes.

    Conciseness, clarity, future orientation, stability, challenge, abstractness, ability to inspire.

    What went wrong at Precision Plastics?

    Bill used S1 on R4 experts. They felt distrusted, slowed down, and one quit.
