    Strategic Marketing Management · Session 1

    Marketing Fundamentals and Customer Value

    Understand the customer. Offer a useful benefit. Make it easy to buy. Measure what customers do and what the business earns.

    Chapter map

    Customer→Need, want, demand→Customer value→Why us?→4Ps (Product, Price, Place, and Promotion)→Funnel response→GET + KEEP→Profit and society

    中文邏輯不是背名詞，而是學「先懂顧客與市場，再決定價值、競爭與 4Ps，最後看顧客反應與商業結果」。

    1. What marketing is

    Key idea

    Marketing means understanding and meeting customer needs and wants. Advertising is only one part of that work.

    Advertising

    One communication tool inside Promotion.

    Marketing

    Customer + value + market + strategy + execution: product, price, distribution, service, branding, research, experience and demand management.

    How it works

    First choose whose problem to solve. Then decide the benefit, price, access and service. Advertising explains this offer. A good ad cannot fix the wrong product or a product people cannot buy.

    Evidence, not common sense

    Example

    Store A sells more iced coffee than Store B. Heat is one possible reason. Customer traffic, price, opening hours and nearby rivals are other reasons. Compare them before choosing an action.假設. Evidence is needed to support a conclusion. Always ask: what other variables could explain the same pattern?

    Observation→Possible explanation≠Proven explanation

    Key idea

    Use common sense to suggest an explanation. Use evidence to choose between explanations.

    Customers are not perfectly rational

    Key idea

    Customers care about more than the lowest price or shortest wait. They also value comfort, identity, status, habits and variety. The best choice depends on the person and situation.

    2. Need, want, demand and customer value

    Need 需要A state of felt deprivation. Hunger.
    ↓ shaped by culture, personality, situation
    Want 想要How the need is expressed. Sushi.
    ↓ plus buying power
    Demand 有效需求A want backed by ability and willingness to pay.

    Key idea

    A need is a basic problem. A want is the preferred way to solve it. Demand exists when the customer is willing and able to pay.

    How it works

    Liking an idea is not enough. A business needs enough customers who will pay for it.

    Customer value

    Customer value ≈ perceived benefits ÷ perceived costs

    Key idea

    Customer value compares the benefits people expect with the costs they expect. Costs include money, time, effort and risk.

    Example

    A coffee costs THB (Thai Baht) 70 but requires a long walk and wait. Another costs THB 100 near the office. A busy customer may choose the second because it saves time. Use the calculator to compare price and time.

    Satisfaction, repeat purchase, loyalty

    Satisfaction

    Was this experience good?

    Repeat purchase

    Did I actually buy again?

    Loyalty

    A stronger, ongoing preference.

    Example

    A customer likes Starbucks but has already had coffee today, wants a change, or finds another shop closer. Satisfaction describes the experience; repeat purchase describes the next choice. Track both.

    3. From product thinking to customer thinking

    Key idea

    Start with the result the customer wants, then choose the product that delivers it. Products can be goods, services, places, people, activities or ideas.

    Product view

    "We sell drills." "We are in the DVD (Digital Versatile Disc) business."

    Customer view

    The customer wants a hole, a finished DIY (Do It Yourself) job. The DVD buyer wants entertainment, so streaming is a competitor.

    Marketing myopia 行銷近視

    Define the business too narrowly→Focus on the current product→Ignore substitutes→Miss environmental change

    Key idea

    Marketing myopia means focusing on today’s product and missing other ways to meet the same need. Ask: what result does the customer want, and what else could provide it?

    Difference is not meaningful differentiation

    Key idea

    A difference matters only when the target customer values it. Test a USP (Unique Selling Proposition) by asking who cares, why it helps, and whether it changes choice or willingness to pay.

    1

    Who cares?

    2

    Why do they care?

    3

    Does it solve a real problem?

    4

    Can competitors easily copy it?

    5

    Would customers pay or switch because of it?

    Me-too marketing

    Firms copy features, products and promotions. In the phone-camera example, customers rarely remember who introduced a feature first. Technical leadership does not guarantee preference.

    Branding

    When features are copied, brand meaning matters more: recognition, associations, expectations and trust. A brand is what customers associate with the offering, not a logo.

    How it works

    Customers must notice the difference, understand the benefit and choose it over alternatives. Features are easy to copy. A clear brand and consistent experience can give people a longer-lasting reason to choose.

    4. Understanding the market

    Attractive for whom?

    Key idea

    A market is attractive for a particular business, target and offer. Always ask: attractive for whom?

    MercedesTesco Lotus
    TargetHigh-income buyersMass households
    Purchase frequencyRare, high valueWeekly, low value
    What matters mostBuying power, statusPopulation size, access, price

    Example

    Mercedes needs buyers who can afford an expensive car, even if they buy rarely. Tesco Lotus needs many households to shop often and reach stores easily. The same population figure means different things to these businesses.

    How it works

    Compare customer needs, income, growth, rivals and access. A large population is useful only if the company can reach people who want and can afford the offer.

    Seasonality and demographics

    Seasonality 季節性

    Weather, holidays, time of day and routines move demand: coffee shops, 7-Eleven mornings, gasoline, taxis near malls. Ask when, where, which customer and why, then adjust inventory, staff, price and hours.

    Demographics

    Age is relatively predictable. An aging Thailand brings threats (shrinking labor force, fewer students) and opportunities (home care, telemedicine, telepharmacy, assisted living, smart homes).

    Trend→Future pain point→Unmet need→Possible opportunity

    External environment

    Key idea

    Watch changes in customers, rivals, the economy, technology, culture, demographics and the natural environment. A good offer can lose its fit when these conditions change.

    How environmental change reaches the 4Ps

    ChangeMechanismPossible response
    Changing customer valuesA once-relevant benefit may matter less as needs, routines and social expectations change.Research whether the value proposition still fits the target.
    RecessionDesire can remain while buying power falls; some wants no longer become demand.Reconsider pack sizes, value tiers and payment/access friction.
    E-commerceSearch and comparison become easier; delivery and returns become part of the offer.Coordinate product information, total price, fulfillment and communication.
    GlobalizationCustomers gain alternatives beyond familiar local competitors.Recheck the benefit and competitive set, including substitutes.

    5. From insight to execution

    The 4Ps as one system

    Product

    What value or solution do we offer?

    Price

    What exchange will customers accept?

    Place

    How and where do customers access it?

    Promotion

    How do customers learn and respond?

    Key idea

    The four parts of the marketing mix must support the same promise. A luxury message clashes with poor service, confusing prices or an unsuitable store.

    The funnel is a diagnostic tool

    Awareness→Interest→Trial→Repeat purchase
    Observed bottleneckWhat it may meanFirst investigation
    Low awarenessRelevant customers may not know the offer exists.Target reach and distribution of the message.
    Aware, little interestThe benefit may seem irrelevant or unclear.Need fit and message understanding.
    Interested, little trialPrice, access, risk or friction may block purchase.Where the first transaction becomes difficult.
    Trial, little repeatThe experience or the next occasion may not support return.Product experience, frequency and alternatives.

    Key idea

    Find the step where customers drop out. Then solve that problem. More advertising will not fix a payment problem or a disappointing product.

    Demand management and GET + KEEP

    Demand management 需求管理

    Too much demand means queues, lower service quality and unhappy customers; too little means idle staff and assets. The goal is appropriate demand at the right time for the capacity. A gym can shift peak users to off-peak hours.

    GET + KEEP

    Demand comes from new and repeat customers. Starting customer base + acquired customers − customers lost = ending customer base, using consistent definitions and periods. 10,000 new customers with 9,500 lost gives a net addition of 500. Session 2 develops acquisition, retention and relationship economics.

    6. Six marketing management philosophies

    PhilosophyStarts withMain risk
    ProductionAffordability and availability: efficiency, distributionIgnores other customer value
    ProductBetter quality and featuresMarketing myopia: better for engineers, not customers
    SellingPersuasion: "we made it, now sell it"Pushes what the firm makes; suits unsought goods
    MarketingTarget customer needs, satisfied better than competitorsRequires real customer orientation
    SocietalCustomer + firm + long-term welfare of societyDifficult trade-offs
    StrategicCustomer value that is also able to earn a profit: logistics, cost, marginsEfficiency without customer fit
    Selling Concept

    Product first → sell harder.

    Marketing Concept

    Customer first → design the offering to fit.

    Why the starting assumption changes the decision

    How it works

    Each philosophy starts with a different question. Production asks how to make the offer cheap and available. Product asks how to improve features. Selling asks how to sell existing output. Marketing starts with customer needs. Societal marketing adds long-term welfare; strategic marketing also checks whether the business can earn a lasting profit.

    Example

    Lower delivery costs can serve two different aims: improve operating efficiency, or make a customer promise easier to deliver. Judge the reason behind the action, not the action alone.

    7. The strategic marketing system

    15Cs (Company, Customers, Competitors, Collaborators, and Context)

    Understand: company, customers, competitors, collaborators, context.

    2STP (Segmentation, Targeting, and Positioning)

    Choose: segmentation, targeting, positioning. Whom to serve, why choose us.

    34Ps

    Execute: deliver and communicate the value.

    Key idea

    Use the 5Cs to understand the situation, STP to choose the customer and position, and the 4Ps to deliver the offer. Positioning gives promotion a clear message and audience.

    Is marketing anti-sustainability?

    Key idea

    Marketing can encourage consumption, but it can also support repair, reuse, recycling and lower waste. The goal is useful value, not simply more units.

    Example

    A disposable product is convenient but creates waste. A repair service can reduce replacement sales while earning service revenue and trust. Compare customer benefit, business return and resource use.

    How it works

    CSR (Corporate Social Responsibility) covers a company’s social and environmental responsibilities. Societal marketing applies this concern to the offer and its long-term effects. A charity message alone does not make daily operations responsible.

    9. Do not mix these up

    Marketing = advertising.

    Promotion is one of four Ps; marketing starts earlier.

    Want = demand.

    Demand needs buying power.

    Satisfied = loyal.

    Satisfied customers still switch.

    Lowest price = highest value.

    Value counts time, effort and risk too.

    Different = differentiated.

    The target must care about the difference.

    Large population = attractive market.

    Attractive for whom, with what fit?

    Low sales = awareness problem.

    Diagnose the funnel stage first.

    More demand is always better.

    Excess demand can hurt service quality.

    Customer orientation ignores profit.

    Strategic marketing includes margins.

    Going online is a strategy.

    A channel is an execution choice.

    10. Cold call practice

    Why do one million ad views not prove marketing success?

    Views show exposure, not trial, repeat purchase, value or profit.

    A hungry person wants omakase but cannot afford it. Need, want, demand?

    Hunger is the need, omakase is the want, and without buying power there is no demand.

    Why can a pricier coffee shop give more value?

    A shorter wait, closer location or less effort can outweigh the higher price for a busy customer.

    What does the drill example teach?

    Customers buy the result, a hole or a finished job, not the product for its own sake.

    What is the risk of defining a business by its current technology?

    Marketing myopia: new solutions can satisfy the same need and take the market.

    Awareness and interest are high but trial is low. What first?

    Check price, availability, perceived risk and purchase friction before buying more awareness.

    How do the Selling, Marketing, Societal and Strategic Concepts differ?

    Selling pushes existing products; Marketing starts with target needs; Societal adds long-run welfare; Strategic also requires efficiency and sustainable profit.

    What does 5Cs → STP → 4Ps mean?

    Understand the situation, choose the target and positioning, then execute the mix.

    Why is lower consumption not necessarily a failure?

    A firm can create value through durability, repair or reuse while building trust and long-run viability.
