    1. Where motivation comes from

    Motivation 動機 is what drives the direction and persistence of effort at work.

    Intrinsic 內在

    Comes from the work itself: challenge, mastery, meaning, enjoyment. An analyst enjoys cracking a hard problem.

    Extrinsic 外在

    Comes from outside rewards or consequences: pay, bonus, promotion, punishment. A salesperson works harder for commission.

    They are not either-or. The useful question is what currently drives behavior, and whether it pushes effort toward the right outcome.

    3. Theory X and Theory Y

    These describe a manager's assumptions 假設 about workers, and those assumptions shape the management style.

    4. Goal setting and MBO

    Management by Objectives (MBO) 目標管理 works only if people know what is expected, by when, and how they are doing.

    Goal specificity

    What counts as success?

    Explicit time period

    By when?

    Participation

    Employees take part in goal decisions.

    Performance feedback

    Am I meeting expectations?

    Slide example of a SMART goal: "Increase customer satisfaction rate by 20% at the end of 2024." It is specific, measurable and time-bound.

    Cascading objectives

    Organization↓Division↓Department↓Individual

    The point is alignment 對齊: hitting an individual KPI should move the department, division and company goals.

    Four problems with goal setting

    1. Goals become ceilings: people stop at the target.
    2. Employees set easy goals to protect themselves.
    3. What gets measured gets done: people chase the KPI even when it is a weak proxy for the real goal.
    4. Hard-to-measure outcomes get ignored: trust, quality, innovation, long-term customer relationships.

    6. Case analysis method

    An issue exists when there is a gap between the framework and workplace reality.

    Symptom 症狀

    Shows something is wrong. "Morale is low."

    Issue 問題核心

    Explains it through a framework. "Supervision and relations are poor (hygiene), and growth opportunities are weak (motivators)."

    Worked example: Company X (Maslow)

    A manufacturer pays well above the industry average and has had zero accidents for years. It focuses on performance, gives employees no chance to build relationships, and pays by the number of products completed each day.

    PhysiologicalNo gapPay above industry average
    SafetyNo gapZero accidents for years

    8. Common mistakes

    Employees lack motivation, so raise pay.

    Diagnose first: needs, style or goals?

    They are well paid, so they should be motivated.

    Pay is a hygiene factor. It removes dissatisfaction only.

    Theory X is bad, Theory Y is good.

    The style must fit the people and the task.

    Decentralized authority means Theory Y.

    Authority can be decentralized while the climate stays control-heavy (LMS).

    Everyone hit their KPI, so the company is doing well.

    The KPI may be a weak proxy for the real goal.

    Frustration-regression happened.

    Show the shift toward lower needs, or state it explicitly as an assumption consistent with the case.

    Use all five theories to look thorough.

    Low morale is the issue.

    It is a symptom. The issue is the framework gap behind it.

    9. Cold call practice

    Answer aloud first, then open the card. Structure: definition, example, why it matters for a manager.

    What is the difference between intrinsic and extrinsic motivation?

    Intrinsic comes from the work itself, such as challenge or meaning. Extrinsic comes from outside rewards or consequences, such as pay or promotion. Both can operate at once.

    What are Maslow's five needs?

    Physiological, safety, social, self-esteem and self-actualization. Lower needs are met before higher ones are pursued.

    How does ERG differ from Maslow?

    ERG groups needs into Existence, Relatedness and Growth, allows several to be pursued at once, and adds frustration-regression.

    What is frustration-regression?

    When a higher need is blocked, the desire for a lower need increases. An employee with no promotion path may focus more on pay.

    Name Herzberg's hygiene factors and motivators.

    Hygiene: supervision, pay, company policies, working conditions, relations with others, job security. Motivators: career advancement, personal growth, recognition, responsibility, achievement.

    Does fixing hygiene factors create motivation?

    No. It only removes dissatisfaction. Motivation requires motivators.

    Are Theory X and Y employee types?

    Mainly they are managers' assumptions about workers, which shape management style.

    What are the four key elements of MBO?

    Goal specificity, explicit time period, participation in decision making, and performance feedback.

    What does "what gets measured gets done" warn about?

    People work to hit the KPI. If the KPI is a weak proxy, the organization may not actually improve.

    When does an issue exist?

    When there is a gap between the framework and workplace reality.

    What are the three ways to motivate people?

    Fulfill human needs, adjust management style to employee characteristics, and set goals.

    10. Case answer: diagnose every part of the framework

    Read the task→Find the criterion→Match facts / assumptions→Assess each component→State the overall issue
    FrameworkWhat a complete analysis covers
    MaslowAssess physiological, safety, belonging, esteem and self-actualization needs separately, with evidence for each.
    ERGAssess existence, relatedness and growth. Use simultaneous needs or frustration-regression when they explain the facts or an explicit premise.
    HerzbergSeparate hygiene conditions that reduce dissatisfaction from motivators that build motivation.
    Theory X / YConnect the manager’s assumptions to the management style, then assess its fit with the task and employees.
    Goal setting / MBOAssess specificity, time period, participation and feedback; identify the missing or weak elements.

    Worked coverage: Maslow applied to LMS
