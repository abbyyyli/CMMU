    Markets and Government

    Prices coordinate markets well, but markets can fail. Government can help, yet every intervention changes incentives, so compare realistic options and their trade-offs.

    Demand Supply Consumer surplus Producer surplus Tax / tariff revenue Deadweight loss

    Chapter map

    Markets workPrices carry information and incentives. Equilibrium maximizes gains from trade.
    →
    Government intervenesTariffs, price ceilings, price floorsResult: less trade, DWL
    Markets failExternalities, public goods, common resourcesResult: too much or too little
    Markets feel wrongMoral economics, repugnance, paternalismResult: restricted or redesigned

    Always askWho responds, and how? What is the trade-off? Could the policy itself fail?

    1. The price system

    Prices solve two problems at once: who knows what, and who has a reason to act.

    A price is a signal wrapped up in an incentive

    Signal

    A rising price tells everyone the good has become scarcer. No one needs to know why.

    Incentive

    The same price change rewards the right response: buyers use less, sellers supply more.

    • Dispersed knowledge 分散的資訊: information about costs, resources and wants is spread across millions of people. No planner holds it all.
    • Information problem: knowing what to produce and where. Incentive problem: getting people to act on it. Prices address both.
    • I, Rose: farmers, air freight and florists each pursue their own income, and roses still reach a cold city on Valentine's Day. This is the invisible hand.

    Outcome and intent are different questions

    The class frame separates intention from result. Markets sit in the unintentional-beneficial cell.

    Beneficial
    Harmful
    Intentional

    Market coordination
    Negligence
    • Adam Smith: the butcher, brewer and baker serve us through self-interest, not benevolence 善意.
    • VHS on eBay: an old player let an elderly buyer recover family videos. Value is subjective 主觀; the seller does not need to know why the buyer values it.

    Equilibrium is a welfare benchmark

    Equilibrium matters for more than Qd = Qs. Up to the equilibrium quantity, willingness to pay (WTP) ≥ marginal cost (MC). Beyond it, MC > WTP.

    2. Trade and tariffs

    A tariff 關稅 raises the domestic price. Producers gain, consumers lose more, and society loses the DWL.

    GroupEffect of the tariff
    Domestic consumersPay more, buy less. CS falls.
    Domestic producersSell more at a higher price. PS rises.
    GovernmentCollects revenue on remaining imports.
    SocietyDeadweight loss from two sources (below).

    Two sources of deadweight loss

    Wasted resources

    High-cost domestic producers replace imports that cost less. Production inefficiency.

    Lost gains from trade

    Some buyers value the good above the world price but stop buying at the tariff price.

    Not all consumer loss is DWL. Part is a transfer 移轉 to producers and government. DWL is the part nobody receives.

    Case: the $10,000 electric car

    The US placed a 100% tariff on Chinese EVs in 2024. Analyze it on two levels and keep them separate:

    Static view

    Today's costs: higher prices, lower consumption, wasted resources, DWL.

    Dynamic view

    Protection may give domestic firms time to scale, learn and secure supply chains.

    The tariff model shows the short-run cost. Whether long-run benefits outweigh it is an empirical question 要看實證, not something the graph can prove.

    3. Price controls

    A binding control stops the price from clearing the market, so scarcity shows up in other ways.

    Price ceiling 最高限價Price floor 最低限價
    RuleLegal maximumLegal minimum
    Binding whenBelow equilibriumAbove equilibrium
    ResultShortage (Qd > Qs)Surplus (Qs > Qd)
    ExampleRent controlMinimum wage

    Five effects of a binding ceiling

    1. Shortage: Qd > Qs.
    2. Lower quality: sellers cut costs they cannot pass on.
    3. Queues and search costs: people pay with time instead of money.
    4. Deadweight loss: trades with WTP > cost disappear.
    5. Misallocation: goods go to whoever queues or has connections, not to those who value them most.

    A low posted price is not a low true cost. Full cost = money price + time + search + lower quality.

    注意投影片把兩種損失分開：wasted time（排隊搜尋）和 A + B（lost CS + lost PS = DWL）。不要混成同一塊。

    Case: rent control in Mumbai

    Rent ceiling below equilibrium→Landlord returns fall→Less maintenance, units withdrawn→Shortage worsens

    The paradox: very cheap legal rent coexists with decaying buildings, vacant units and a housing shortage.

    Blood plasma and outlawed markets

    • Blood plasma 血漿: banning donor pay acts like a price fixed at zero. Supply stays low, need stays high, so there is a shortage and DWL. This is an efficiency cost; the moral question needs separate analysis.
    • Outlawed markets: the slide shows both demand and supply shifting inward. Legal risk lowers participation on both sides. Quantity falls; the price effect is ambiguous 不確定 because it depends on which curve shifts more.

    4. Externalities

    An externality 外部性 is a cost or benefit that falls on a third party outside the trade.

    Social cost = Private cost + External cost
    Social benefit = Private benefit + External benefit

    Negative externality: the market produces too much

    Decision-makers see only private cost→Cost looks too low→Q market > Q efficient→DWL from overuse
    • Antibiotics (MRU): overuse breeds resistant bacteria, a cost borne by future patients.
    • Thailand PM2.5: private activity creates air-pollution costs for people outside the trade. The slide does not name one source, so treat it as an illustration.
    • Sugar external-cost graph: MSC above MPC, so the market overuses. A classic externality needs a third-party cost; harm to the consumer alone is not one.

    Policy tools for negative externalities

    ToolHow it worksTrade-off
    Pigouvian tax 庇古稅Tax equal to marginal external cost, so decision-makers face the full social cost.Needs an estimate of the external cost.
    Command and controlGovernment sets rules directly, such as an emission limit or required technology.Simple but costly, because firms' reduction costs differ.
    Tradable permitsGovernment caps total pollution. Firms that can cut cheaply cut more and sell permits.Meets the cap at the lowest total cost. Permits do not mean more pollution; the cap is fixed.
    Coase bargainingWith clear property rights and cheap bargaining, the parties solve it themselves.Fails when many people are affected (PM2.5 hits millions).

    Positive externality: the market produces too little

    • Vaccination: protects the person and lowers infection risk for others.
    • COVID vaccine development: the social value of fast vaccines far exceeded what firms could earn, so advance purchase commitments and R&D support made sense.
    • Tools: subsidies, public provision, prizes, purchase commitments.

    5. Public goods and common resources

    Classify a good by two questions: can non-payers be excluded, and does one person's use reduce what others get?

    Rival 競爭性
    Nonrival
    Excludable 排他性
    Private goodFood, clothing
    Club goodNetflix, Spotify
    Nonexcludable
    Common resourceOcean tuna. Problem: overuse
    Public goodAsteroid defense, national defense. Problem: free riders
    • Free rider 搭便車: people enjoy a public good without paying, so voluntary funding falls short.
    • Forced rider: tax funding makes some people pay for things they value below their cost.
    • A public good is defined by its traits, not by who provides it.

    Tragedy of the commons

    Each fisher keeps the full gain from one more fish but shares the cost of a smaller stock with everyone. Each choice is rational; the total is overuse.

    Elinor Ostrom showed that communities can manage commons with their own rules, monitoring and sanctions. The choice is not only market versus government.

    Other fixes: property rights, quotas, tradable fishing permits.

    6. Moral economics

    A voluntary, mutually beneficial trade can still be socially unacceptable. Efficiency is one criterion, not the only one.

    ConceptMeaningExample
    Repugnance 反感People reject a trade because paying for it feels wrong, even if both sides agree.Kidney sales
    FramingThe same exchange is accepted or rejected depending on how it is structured.Tax credit for donors instead of cash; life insurance framed as protecting family
    Domestic ban, foreign supplyBanning a market at home does not end reliance on it.Countries that ban paid plasma import it from paid donors abroad
    Hidden economicsSome "moral" objections are also economic problems.Subscription firefighting: fire spreads (externality) and people expect rescue anyway (free rider)
    Viatical contractSelling a life insurance policy early for cash. The buyer profits if the seller dies sooner.Patients during the AIDS crisis
    Paternalism 父愛主義Limiting choices to protect people from harming themselves.Prescriptions; self-set loss limits on betting apps

    Externality: does my choice hurt others? Paternalism: will I hurt myself?

    Beyond allow or ban: market design

    Change the form of payment, who may participate, or set limits. Examples: kidney exchange matching, rules on who may hold a life policy, loss caps on gambling.

    1. Is there a potential gain from trade?
    2. Why is the market contested?
    3. What happens if it is banned or restricted? (Demand moves abroad or underground.)
    4. Can the institution be redesigned?
    5. Evaluate more than efficiency.

    7. Taxes, subsidies and government failure

    Who legally pays a tax does not decide who bears it. Elasticity does.

    • Tax incidence 租稅歸宿: the less elastic side bears more. Cigarette buyers are inelastic, so they carry most of a cigarette tax.
    • Subsidy: a negative tax that encourages an activity, such as EV purchase subsidies.
    • Government failure: the policy itself produces an inefficient result. Compare realistic market outcomes with realistic government outcomes, not ideal versions of either.

    8. Common mistakes

    A high price means market failure.

    A high price may simply signal scarcity.

    A price ceiling makes goods affordable for everyone.

    A binding ceiling causes a shortage. Some people get nothing.

    All consumer loss from a tariff is DWL.

    Part is transferred to producers and government.

    DWL always means too little trade.

    A negative externality creates DWL from too much trade.

    Pollution permits allow more pollution.

    The cap is fixed. Trading only decides who cuts.

    Anything shared is a public good.

    A fishery is rival, so it is a common resource.

    The tragedy of the commons comes from irrational people.

    Each user is rational. The cost is shared.

    Banning a market removes demand.

    Demand moves abroad, to imports or to black markets.

    Paternalism is the same as an externality.

    Externality harms others. Paternalism protects people from themselves.

    The only options are allow or ban.

    Market design can change payment form, eligibility or limits.

    9. Cold call practice

    Answer aloud first, then open the card. Structure: definition, example, why it matters.

    What does "a price is a signal wrapped up in an incentive" mean?

    A price tells people a good has become scarcer and gives them a reason to act: buyers use less, sellers supply more.

    What is the invisible hand?

    People pursuing their own interests are coordinated by prices into outcomes that serve others, without a central planner.

    Why is equilibrium more than Qd = Qs?

    Up to the equilibrium quantity WTP ≥ MC, and beyond it MC > WTP. Equilibrium therefore maximizes total surplus in the basic model.

    Who wins and loses from a tariff?

    Domestic producers gain, consumers lose, government collects revenue, and society loses DWL from wasted resources and lost gains from trade.

    What is deadweight loss?

    The value lost because the traded quantity differs from the efficient quantity. Nobody receives it.

    Why can Mumbai have vacant flats and a housing shortage?

    Rent control cuts landlord returns, so owners reduce maintenance and withhold units. Supply falls even though controlled rent is low.

    How does wasted time differ from A + B?

    Wasted time is the real cost of queuing and searching. A + B is lost consumer and producer surplus from trades that no longer happen.

    Why does a negative externality cause overproduction?

    Decision-makers ignore the external cost, so they keep trading past the point where social cost exceeds benefit. Q market > Q efficient.

    How do tradable permits work?

    Government caps total pollution. Firms that can cut cheaply do so and sell permits, so the cap is met at the lowest total cost.

    When does the Coase theorem fail?

    When property rights are unclear or bargaining is costly, for example when millions of people are affected by PM2.5.

    Why is a fishery not a public good?

    It is nonexcludable but rival: one person's catch reduces what others can catch. That makes it a common resource.

    What did Elinor Ostrom show?

    Communities can govern common resources with their own rules, monitoring and sanctions, without full privatization or central control.

    Why might a voluntary trade be unacceptable?

    Repugnance: people object to paying for certain things, such as kidneys, even when both sides gain.

    Besides morality, what problems does subscription firefighting create?

    A negative externality, because fire spreads to neighbors, and a free-rider problem, because people expect to be saved anyway.

    What happens when a market is outlawed?

    Both demand and supply can shift inward. Quantity falls, while the price effect depends on which shift is larger.

    Who bears the burden of a tax?

    The less elastic side of the market, regardless of who legally pays it.
