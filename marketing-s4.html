    1. Strategic planning: the plan must reach the customer

    Key idea

    Strategic planning connects the company’s goals, abilities and market opportunities. It gives teams a shared direction.

    Vision→Mission→Objectives→Department plans→Frontline behavior→Feedback

    How it works

    Leaders set the long-term direction. Managers turn it into goals and budgets. Frontline teams need clear standards, training and resources. Feedback shows what needs to change.

    Vision

    Where do we want to go?

    Future
    Mission

    Why do we exist, and what customer need do we serve?

    Purpose + value
    Objectives

    What will change, by how much and by when?

    Market-oriented mission and brand fit

    Key idea

    Define the business by the customer need, not just today’s product. “Help offices work better” gives more room to adapt than “sell photocopiers.”

    Example

    Malee’s “The Fruit Expert” promise suggests fruit expertise. A mushroom drink must still make sense under that promise. The company can use the main brand, a sub-brand or a different brand, depending on the fit.

    How it works

    A useful mission guides which opportunities to pursue and what customers should experience.

    2. BCG (Boston Consulting Group): classify the business, then judge the option

    Key idea

    BCG compares market growth with relative market share. It helps organize choices about where to put resources.

    How it works

    Read the two axes separately. A fast-growing market can contain a small brand. A large brand can operate in a slow market. Use profit, cash needs and company ability to decide what to do next.

    Interactive · BCG positioning lab

    Move the two sliders or drag the point. Watch the quadrant change, but do not let the quadrant make the decision for you.

    Star

    More detailLab settings: high growth starts at 10%; strong relative share starts at 1.0×. Higher share is on the left.

    Why the quadrant is not an automatic instruction

    PositionInterpretationManagement question
    StarFast-growing market; strong relative positionCan we fund growth efficiently and protect our lead?
    Cash CowSlow-growth market; strong relative positionHow much cash can the business generate without eroding strength?
    Question MarkFast-growing market; weak positionCan we realistically gain share?
    DogSlow-growth market; weak positionIs there niche, strategic or future value worth keeping?
    OptionMeaningEvidence needed
    BuildInvest to gain share.A feasible path to stronger share and enough future returns.
    HoldSupport the current competitive position.What maintenance prevents erosion and at what cost.
    HarvestLimit new investment while collecting remaining cash where appropriate.Whether reduced support damages more valuable future business.
    DivestSell or withdraw from the business.Alternatives, exit costs and any niche or complementary value.

    Example

    Class examples link Breeze with a Cash Cow, home phones or pagers with a Dog, EV (Electric Vehicle) models with a Question Mark and smartphones with a Star. Use each to practice reading market growth and relative share. A mature Coca-Cola or Pepsi still needs brand support.

    How it works

    Build, hold, harvest and divest are choices. Compare the return, funding and strategic role before deciding.

    中文邏輯市場高成長 ≠ 本公司市占率高；市占率高 ≠ 一定獲利。BCG 幫你思考資源分配，不會自動給唯一答案。

    Product Life Cycle · different stages, different management questions

    Key idea

    PLC (Product Life Cycle) follows sales over time: introduction, growth, maturity and decline. Each stage changes the questions about awareness, access, repeat purchase, cost and renewal.

    PLC asks: what stage?

    The Product Life Cycle describes how a category's sales may develop over time. It is a useful pattern, not a guaranteed timetable.

    BCG asks: what position?

    The matrix combines market growth with our relative competitive share. A mature category is not automatically a Cash Cow for every brand.

    中文邏輯PLC 描述市場或產品可能經歷的生命週期；BCG 還要看「本公司的相對市占率」。市場成熟不代表每個品牌都成了金牛事業。

    BCG decision check · A Star is losing money. Must we invest?

    Not automatically. First check whether losses come from temporary growth investment or an unviable business model. Compare future margins, funding needs, competitive advantage and alternatives. Quadrant → hypotheses → evidence → decision, not quadrant → command.

    SWOT and environmental scanning

    Internal: Strengths / Weaknesses

    Resources, capabilities, execution and constraints inside the firm.

    External: Opportunities / Threats

    Competition, technology, demographics, politics, culture and changing customer needs.

    How it works

    Turn the four boxes into an action. A retailer can use reliable delivery, an internal strength, to serve a growing area, an external opportunity. Set a clear goal and use the strength to reach it.

    Key idea

    Generation labels depend on local history. The same birth years in Thailand, China and the United States can involve different technology, events and routines.

    Example

    Older customers may use online banking when its convenience outweighs their concerns. Kodak shows a related planning lesson: customers still want to keep memories when the technology changes from film.

    How it works

    SWOT supports a strategy when each factor leads to a realistic action and a clear goal.

    4. Buyer Decision Process: a guide, not a compulsory ritual

    Key idea

    The consumer’s “black box” includes motives, experience, social context and perception. These shape the response to a marketing offer.

    StageWhat happensMarketing implication
    Need recognitionA gap or stimulus makes a need salient.Connect the offer to a real problem or occasion.
    Information searchUse memory, reviews, people or trial to learn.Make relevant and credible information accessible.
    EvaluationCompare feasible options on benefits that matter here.Understand the actual shortlist and trade-offs.
    PurchaseChoose and obtain an option.Reduce access, payment and availability friction.
    PostpurchaseEvaluate, recommend, complain, repeat or dispose.Deliver the promise and support the next experience.

    Interactive · How the five stages change

    Switch situations. Select a stage to see why it is long, brief or sometimes skipped.

    These are plausible paths, not fixed empirical stage durations. The professor's Volvo anecdote illustrates an exception; it does not mean all car buying is impulsive.

    Example

    At a Volvo event, the professor test-drove a red car and bought it despite having no earlier plan to buy. Familiarity, emotion and the occasion can shorten the search and evaluation stages, even for an expensive purchase.

    Who is buying? Demographics, psychographics and self-image

    Demographics

    Age, family status, income, location: who the buyer is.

    Psychographics

    Values, interests, lifestyle, identity: why the benefit matters.

    Example

    Corvette and station-wagon exercises show how a car suggests a lifestyle or desired self-image. Brand image describes an association, rather than every owner’s age or family status.

    TrapVALS and other segments organize hypotheses; they do not reveal each person's entire identity. 品牌印象 ≠ 真實買家的人口統計。

    中文抓重點人口統計能描述「誰」，但無法單靠年齡預測「為什麼買」。教授用跑車與旅行車讓你看到品牌形象和真實車主可能完全不同。

    Benefit segmentation and self-image

    Key idea

    Benefit segmentation groups people by what they want from the offer. One hotel guest wants luxury and atmosphere; another wants a clean room near transport. This changes the product, price and message.

    How it works

    Demographics describe people. Psychographics describe values and lifestyles. Self-image is how people see themselves or wish to be seen. VALS (Values and Lifestyles) is one framework for organizing lifestyle differences.

    5. Motivation, involvement, risk and postpurchase behavior

    Key idea

    One purchase can meet several needs: function, safety, belonging, status or personal growth. Involvement depends on how important and risky the decision feels.

    Need / context→Importance & perceived risk→Information search→Choice→Postpurchase feeling
    Do not mix upWhat it means
    InvolvementHow important or risky the decision seems before/during purchase. Price alone does not determine it.
    Perceived riskExpected consequences: financial, functional, social or safety concerns.
    Cognitive dissonanceUncomfortable doubt after choosing between appealing alternatives.
    DiffusionHow an innovation spreads across adopters with different risk and benefit perceptions.

    Example

    Water for instant coffee may be a routine choice. Water for preparing infant formula can feel more important to the buyer. The use changes the how important the choice feels.

    Example

    A buyer likes a Louis Vuitton bag but keeps thinking about Hermès. This is doubt about a missed alternative: cognitive dissonance after purchase.

    How it works

    Adoption depends on perceived benefit, risk, ease of use and fit with existing habits. Buying early and influencing others are different roles.

    Maslow's needs · several motives can operate at once

    Key idea

    Several Maslow needs can act at the same time. Use the categories to ask better questions about the buying occasion.

    Example

    A restaurant meal can meet hunger, safety, friendship and status needs together. The occasion changes which benefit matters most.

    中文邏輯同一餐可能同時滿足吃飽、安全、社交與面子需求。不要把 Maslow 當成「第一層完全滿足才能進入第二層」的固定規則。

    Diffusion of Innovations · early buyer vs. influential buyer

    Key idea

    Some buyers try new products early. Others wait for better reliability, support or prices. An opinion leader influences choices; a lead user has advanced needs that can guide product ideas.

    How it works

    An early buyer is not always influential. Find both the people willing to try and the people whose advice others trust.

    Adoption roleMeaningDo not confuse with
    Innovator / early adopterTries relatively early and accepts some uncertainty.Automatic influence on other buyers.
    Opinion leaderOthers trust their judgment or experience.Simply being first to buy.
    Lead userAdvanced needs can reveal future product requirements.A typical mass-market user.
    Later adopterWaits for better evidence, price, compatibility or support.A fixed lack of ability or interest.

    Example

    A microwave can feel unfamiliar or risky. CD (Compact Disc) recordings offered easier track selection and no rewinding, but replacing a collection cost money. Buyers weigh these gains and costs.

    How it works

    After a hard choice, clear onboarding and helpful support can reassure a buyer. Fixing a faulty product is different from easing doubt about another attractive option.

    6. Coffee choice: which benefit matters in this situation?

    Key idea

    Utilitarian benefits help a task. Hedonic benefits give enjoyment or experience. Coffee can offer caffeine, taste, convenience, atmosphere and social time at once.功能性價值, hedonic/social benefits 情感／體驗價值, taste and convenience can all coexist. Their importance changes with the purchase occasion.

    Example

    On a workday, speed may matter most. With friends, atmosphere may matter more. At home, taste may lead. The convertible-car ad uses a straw to sell a cool, refreshing feeling instead of listing technical features.

    Interactive · Change the coffee context

    The weights sum to 100. Compare which benefit leads in each situation.

    Creative message vs. measurable marketing outcome

    Key idea

    A memorable ad must connect its idea to the brand and a useful benefit. Choose a measure that matches the goal: attention, brand recall, consideration, purchase or profit.

    Example

    Xcite’s wet-dog breath-mint image attracts attention. If people remember only bad breath, the ad may help the category. If they remember Xcite and its benefit, it can help the brand. Heineken with friends also shows how social context changes choice.

    7. Brand awareness is not the same as consideration or purchase

    Key idea

    In this class, “evoked set” means known brands. The consideration set contains brands the customer would seriously choose. Awareness must become consideration before it can support purchase.

    Interactive · Brand-choice funnel

    Start with 100 potential shoppers. Change what share knows the brand, what share of the aware would consider it, and what share of considerers purchase.

    The rates apply at each step: awareness among all shoppers, consideration among aware shoppers, purchase among considerers.

    Decision practice · what would convince you?

    Key idea

    Start with the buying occasion. At home, price and convenience may lead. At a public event, presentation may matter more. Compare the benefits important to that customer.

    Case A · A consumer says: “I buy premium water because it tastes better.” What can we conclude?

    Evidence: the consumer reports a taste preference. Not yet proven: that taste objectively explains the premium purchase or predicts other consumers. A blind taste test and an occasion-specific choice study could help distinguish sensory preference from branding and social context.

    Case B · A bottled-water advertisement gets lots of views. Did the campaign succeed?

    It depends on the objective. Views may support an awareness goal, but cannot by themselves prove brand attribution, consideration or sales. Choose the outcome first, then the relevant metric; compare results with a suitable baseline if making a causal claim.

    10. Cold call & Class Discussion practice

    Try saying the answer in English before opening it.

    What makes a mission different from an objective?

    A mission explains whom the organization serves and the value it aims to create. Objectives turn that direction into measurable results, deadlines and actions.

    Why doesn't a Star automatically deserve more investment?

    BCG describes growth and relative market position, but investment still depends on profitability, cash needs, capabilities and feasible future returns.

    What is Ansoff's diversification strategy?

    Entering a new market with a new product. It differs from product development, where the market already exists, and market development, where the product already exists.

    Why can a car buyer skip stages in the Buyer Decision Process?

    The five stages are a conceptual model. Prior experience, urgency, emotion or an impulse can shorten search or evaluation even in a high-priced category.

    What is the difference between demographics and psychographics?

    Demographics describe characteristics such as age or income. Psychographics examine values, lifestyle and motives that may better explain choices.

    Why is postpurchase dissonance different from dissatisfaction?

    A buyer may like a product while wondering whether a forgone alternative would have been better. Dissatisfaction is usually about perceived poor product or service performance.

    How should a marketer analyze premium bottled water?

    I would define the segment and buying occasion, identify the important functional, risk-related and symbolic benefits, then test which message improves consideration or trial rather than assuming premium means healthier.

    What was the marketing weakness of the Xcite creative example?

    A memorable idea may not link to the advertised brand. We should measure brand attribution, recall and relevant consumer response, not just attention.

    Why can't we copy U.S. generational cohorts directly into Thailand?

    The experiences defining a cohort depend on local history, culture and technology. Birth-year labels alone do not establish the same motives or behavior in a different market.
