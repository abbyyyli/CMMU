    Managing People in Organizations · Case

    Lee Medical Supply

    A financial turnaround that worked, and a people system that broke. The case runs across sessions: Session 3 diagnoses the issues, Session 4 recommends fixes, and Session 5 adds EQ diagnosis and executive coaching.

    S3 · Diagnose

    Explain the motivation gap with Maslow, Herzberg, Theory X/Y and goal setting.

    S4 · Design improvements

    Link each recommendation to a mechanism, a measure and a practical trade-off.

    S5 · Coach the leader

    Use the new incidents to identify EQ gaps and practice better leadership behavior.

    1. The case at a glance

    KP Industries buys distressed companies and installs new leaders to turn them around. Lee Medical Supply (LMS) distributes medical items, with sales of about $300 million and net earnings of about $12 million.

    Jan 4, 2025Apichart appointed President

    Task: bring sales and profit up to KP standards. Review planned after 18 months.

    Jul 20, 2025CEO Krisada reviews results

    Finances meet KP standards, but a consultant report shows serious people and strategic problems.

    Aug 6, 2025New expectations

    Apichart must deliver a plan within two weeks to cure the "corporate cancer". First review after six months.

    The handout's dates are about 6.5 months apart but the text says 18 months. Refer to "the stated 18-month turnaround" and do not guess which is wrong.

    KPI in this case can mean KP Industries, the parent company, not key performance indicator.

    Scoreboard

    Financial
    • Sales up
    • Profit up
    • Timely, accurate reports
    • Accountability up
    People
    • 3 of 9 VPs resigned, 4 terminated
    • Well-paid, well-trained staff leaving
    • Low morale
    • Training and career plans stopped
    • Too little feedback
    Customer
    • Formal complaints rising
    • Survey results worse
    Innovation
    • No new products in 12 months
    • New-product rate at an all-time low

    The central tension: Apichart optimized financial performance, but the people system and broader strategic results declined. This is a trade-off problem, not "Apichart is bad" or "the old culture was good".

    2. Two sides of the story

    Consultant and employees

    Written orders, intolerance of mistakes, unwillingness to discuss problems. Managers "only interested in numbers". Tense competition replaced a collegial, family-oriented atmosphere.

    Apichart

    He inherited "a happy but unproductive workplace": 22-year average tenure, low technical skills, seniority-based promotion, straight salaries, tolerance of poor performance. Progress has costs; his job is results.

    What Apichart changed

    PeopleReplaced managers he judged incompetent; encouraged mediocre staff to leave
    StructureDivisions, regions and districts; each unit a profit center; staffing and pay authority decentralized
    ControlPerformance review department, better accounting, resources allocated by performance
    GoalsManagement by objectives (MBO), performance standards, required appraisals
    RewardsProfit-based bonuses, sales commissions, incentives tied to measurable results
    CultureInternal competition made formal policy

    The CEO's new definition of success

    Financial performance+Lower turnover among high performers+More customer influence on operations+Better new-product development

    3. Evidence discipline

    Strong answers separate what the case states from what we infer.

    Stated in the case

    Turnover; leavers well paid; training stopped; weak feedback; no new products; rising complaints; bonuses, commissions and mandated competition exist.

    Reasonable inference

    Relationships and growth opportunities weakened; financial KPIs may be too narrow; the climate may discourage ideas.

    Cannot conclude

    That low pay caused turnover; that bonuses alone caused competition; that staff fear speaking up (this is only the CEO's suspicion); the exact reward schedule.

    7. Session 5: EQ and executive coaching

    Human cost 人際代價

    9. An integrated plan: keep the gains, repair the gaps

    A
    Diagnose the causes.

    Separate resignations from terminations; use confidential interviews to check needs, job design, rewards and supervision.

    B
    Repair the system and behavior.

    Clarify shared goals, restore feedback and development, and coach private correction and listening.

    C
    Align incentives.

    Keep financial accountability; also recognize customer problem-solving, teamwork, learning and innovation.

    D
    Evaluate, then scale.

    Compare the baseline with similar units or periods. Track leadership behavior and the CEO’s outcomes together.

    10. Do not mix these up

    S3High pay means motivation needs are met.

    Pay addresses only part of the problem. Stopped development and weakened relationships point to unmet growth and belonging needs.

    S3Decentralized authority proves a Theory Y approach.

    Formal authority was delegated, but intolerance of mistakes and imposed competition keep the management climate control-heavy.

    S4Any employee departure is Exit in EVLN.

    Resignation can be an Exit response. A terminated employee did not choose to leave; keep the two groups separate.

    S4Rewarding sales automatically improves teamwork and customers.

    Rewards strengthen the behavior they pay for. Include cooperation and customer problem-solving when these are required outcomes.

    S5Respectful correction means lowering the standard.

    Correct the report and preserve accountability. Change the public insult, dismissive response and rejection of feedback.

    S5An organized manager’s personality label measures EQ.

    J-like execution describes a working preference. EQ analysis examines emotional awareness, regulation and the effect on relationships.

    11. Cold call practice

    S3 · Motivation diagnosis

    Why is low pay an incomplete explanation for turnover?

    Many leavers are capable, trained and well paid. Examine supervision, relationships, feedback and growth as well as pay.

    What does Herzberg distinguish in this case?

    Supervision and relationships are hygiene factors. Development, recognition and responsibility are motivators. Improve both sides.

    Which goal-setting gaps come from the handout and the recap?

    The handout reports weak performance feedback. The recap adds that staff do not know the overall profit goal.

    S4 · System improvements

    Why keep some of Apichart’s controls?

    They improved sales, profit, reporting and accountability. Repair the people system while preserving those gains.

    How can EVLN connect the original and new narratives?

    Voluntary resignations are Exit. Syamol first offers constructive Voice and later leaves. Terminated VPs are a separate group.

    What should a reward redesign reinforce?

    Financial accountability plus customer problem-solving, cooperation, learning and innovation. Specify the reward rule before naming its schedule.

    S5 · EQ and coaching

    Which EQ factors are most directly implicated by insult, ignored grief and rejected feedback?

    Self-control, empathy and social expertise, respectively. Link each diagnosis to the specific behavior.

    What can personality analysis add without replacing EQ analysis?

    Organized execution supports a J-like preference. It does not explain humiliation; assess emotional conduct and awareness of impact separately.

    How would you check whether coaching is working?

    Observe repeated private correction, listening and follow-through; collect confidential employee feedback; track retention, customers, innovation and finance together.

    Integrated answer (60 to 90 seconds)

    LMS improved sales, profit and accountability, but lost capable people while customer and innovation outcomes weakened. In S3 terms, stopped development and damaged relationships weaken growth and belonging; poor feedback and unclear shared goals reduce direction. A control-heavy style also limits initiative. For S4, keep financial discipline while restoring development, regular feedback and participation; align rewards with teamwork, customer problem-solving and innovation. For S5, coach Apichart to pause, correct errors privately, acknowledge distress and receive criticism constructively. Connect these behaviors to a shared direction beyond profit. Evaluate respectful conduct and employee experience alongside high-performer retention, customer responsiveness, product development and margin. The plan joins motivation, organizational systems and leadership behavior.
