    1. The case at a glance

    KP Industries buys distressed companies and installs new leaders to turn them around. Lee Medical Supply (LMS) distributes medical items, with sales of about $300 million and net earnings of about $12 million.

    Jan 4, 2025Apichart appointed President

    Task: bring sales and profit up to KP standards. Review planned after 18 months.

    Jul 20, 2025CEO Krisada reviews results

    Finances meet KP standards, but a consultant report shows serious people and strategic problems.

    Aug 6, 2025New expectations

    Apichart must deliver a plan within two weeks to cure the "corporate cancer". First review after six months.

    The handout's dates are about 6.5 months apart but the text says 18 months. Refer to "the stated 18-month turnaround" and do not guess which is wrong.

    KPI in this case can mean KP Industries, the parent company, not key performance indicator.

    Scoreboard

    Financial
    • Sales up
    • Profit up
    • Timely, accurate reports
    • Accountability up
    People
    • 3 of 9 VPs resigned, 4 terminated
    • Well-paid, well-trained staff leaving
    • Low morale
    • Training and career plans stopped
    • Too little feedback
    Customer
    • Formal complaints rising
    • Survey results worse
    Innovation
    • No new products in 12 months
    • New-product rate at an all-time low

    The central tension: Apichart optimized financial performance, but the people system and broader strategic results declined. This is a trade-off problem, not "Apichart is bad" or "the old culture was good".

    2. Two sides of the story

    Consultant and employees

    Written orders, intolerance of mistakes, unwillingness to discuss problems. Managers "only interested in numbers". Tense competition replaced a collegial, family-oriented atmosphere.

    Apichart

    He inherited "a happy but unproductive workplace": 22-year average tenure, low technical skills, seniority-based promotion, straight salaries, tolerance of poor performance. Progress has costs; his job is results.

    What Apichart changed

    PeopleReplaced managers he judged incompetent; encouraged mediocre staff to leave
    StructureDivisions, regions and districts; each unit a profit center; staffing and pay authority decentralized
    ControlPerformance review department, better accounting, resources allocated by performance
    GoalsManagement by objectives (MBO), performance standards, required appraisals
    RewardsProfit-based bonuses, sales commissions, incentives tied to measurable results
    CultureInternal competition made formal policy

    The CEO's new definition of success

    Financial performance+Lower turnover among high performers+More customer influence on operations+Better new-product development

    10. Do not mix these up

    High pay satisfies all motivation needs.

    Pay can coexist with poor relationships, disrespect and blocked growth. Use need categories and Herzberg’s two-factor distinction to locate the gap.

    One management style fits everyone.

    Chalermsak’s routine preference supports clear instructions, not an automatic X classification. X/Y concerns assumptions about motivation and responsibility; structured goals can also support Theory Y.

    A numerical target is a complete goal system.

    MBO needs specificity, an explicit time period, participation and feedback. Set, monitor and manage; check whether rewards support the intended outcome.

    EQ coaching alone repairs the whole company.

    Respectful leaders still need sound jobs, development, goals and rewards. Join behavioral practice to the organizational changes required by the diagnosis.

    Naming more theories makes an answer stronger.

    Use every framework required by the question and complete its components. A chosen main theory needs a full evidence-to-action argument, not a list of theory names.

    Lower turnover proves higher motivation and commitment.

    Separate resignations from terminations. Check job feelings, commitment, Voice and voluntary helping directly, alongside business results.

    11. Cold call practice

    How do you choose the main theory when the question leaves the choice open?

    Choose the framework whose mechanism best explains the strongest facts. State the issue it explains, then trace evidence to the gap and action.

    How can Maslow explain high-paid employees leaving?

    Pay addresses physiological needs. Competition weakens belonging, public insult undermines esteem and stopped development restricts self-actualization. Check safety concerns rather than assume them.

    How does Herzberg sharpen a needs-based answer?

    Fix poor supervision and relationships to reduce dissatisfaction; restore development, recognition and responsibility to build motivation. These are different tasks.

    What does X/Y add beyond “give everyone autonomy”?

    Examine managerial assumptions and their effect on control. Keep useful structure for routine work and add bounded participation where initiative is needed.

    How do goals and reinforcement work together?

    Goals specify direction, time and feedback; rewards shape the behavior pursued. Align both with finance, customers, retention and innovation.

    What makes an EQ recommendation actionable?

    Name the trigger, factor and replacement behavior: when a report is weak, pause, correct factual errors privately and agree follow-up. Practice across leadership levels.

    How can EVLN and OCB help evaluate an intervention?

    Track constructive Voice, voluntary resignations and voluntary helping separately. Syamol’s criticism is Voice; her later departure is Exit. Neither alone measures every attitude.

    Worked answers: complete the requested components

    Main theory: Maslow

    Using Maslow, pay provides support for physiological needs. Safety is a possible gap if intolerance of mistakes makes staff fear arbitrary treatment; under that assumption, clarify review and appeal steps and check perceived fairness. Mandated competition weakens belonging, so assign a shared customer project and review cooperation. Esteem is partly supported by performance rewards, but the consultant reports little respect for employee feelings and needs; require respectful feedback and recognize customer and team contributions. Stopped training and career plans restrict self-actualization; restore a development task and mentor, with a completed learning output. The overall diagnosis is that pay support coexists with relationship, respect and growth gaps. The CEO should review those changes alongside voluntary high-performer resignations and financial performance.

    Main theory: Theory X / Y

    Using Theory X/Y, the problem is an assumption that employees mainly need control and external pressure, reflected in written orders, intolerance of mistakes and rejection of feedback. This restricts the initiative required for customer service and innovation. A Theory Y approach would assume capable staff can accept responsibility and self-direct under clear shared goals. I would delegate defined customer decisions, involve staff in goal setting and review quality and results. Chalermsak’s preference for routine supports clear instructions, but does not classify him as X; clarity can serve either approach. Leaders should explain decision rights, listen without retaliation and correct mistakes privately. Agree goals and review dates, recognize cooperation, and check customer resolution, constructive Voice and financial performance. The change replaces blanket control with supported responsibility while preserving accountability.

    Session 5: personality, five EQ factors and coaching

    Assuming the reported work habits reflect preferences, I propose ISTJ: low outward interaction suggests I, detail and profit focus S, task-based criteria T, and planned control J. He knows his management beliefs but misses their effect on staff, so impact awareness needs development. Factor 1: yelling at Syamol and rejecting feedback show a regulation and awareness gap; use a weekly incident review and private factual correction, checked through confidential feedback. Factor 2: continuing during grief shows an empathy gap; acknowledge the loss, agree support and follow up within two working days. Factor 3: avoiding relationship activity and rejecting criticism show weak social expertise; hold weekly listening meetings with owners and response dates for concerns. Factor 4: reliance on authority weakens personal influence; explain decisions, recognize specific contributions and support one staff-led improvement each month. Factor 5: the narrow profit message leaves a vision gap; agree shared purpose and decision principles, then connect them to the four outcomes and unit goals in the monthly staff session. Put every task into his performance plan, with a named reviewer and weekly checks. Use the coach to test the replaceability belief against the CEO’s retention goal. My conclusion covers all five gaps: regulate the response, understand employees, repair relationships, build willing support and communicate shared direction, while retaining financial accountability.
