    1. What culture is and what it does

    “Culture is the way we do things around here, or what keeps the herd moving in roughly the right direction.” (Deal and Kennedy, 1982)

    Put simply, culture is what people really do, not what the rulebook says. It keeps everyone moving in roughly the same direction without a manager pushing each person.

    The deck also mentions sustainability culture: the shared beliefs and habits about a company's impact on the environment. The slide says culture matters most during change (Assoratgoon and Kantabutra, 2023).

    Function (p.5)In plain wordsExample
    Creates corporate identityTells staff and customers who “we” areStaff and customers describe the company the same way
    Creates meaning and commitmentShows why the work mattersPeople stay because they believe in what the company does
    Sets expectations and standardsShows what good work looks likeNew staff learn the quality bar from coworkers
    Helps people make sense of eventsHelps people read what is happeningAfter a reorganization, people know what it means for them
    Shapes behavior without supervisionGuides what people do when nobody checksA night-shift worker fixes a customer problem without being told

    Slide p.6 calls culture a way of controlling behavior without formal rules. Staff pass the unwritten rules to each other. Public and private organizations both have a culture.

    Structure (Session 7)

    Formal control: the chart of who reports to whom and who can talk to whom.

    Culture (Session 8)

    Informal control: what people do because “that is how we work here.”

    容易混淆S6 的 national culture 是一個國家的平均傾向；這堂的 corporate culture 是一家公司的做事方式。兩者都是共享、學來的，但層次不同。

    2. How culture forms

    • From the top: the founders' beliefs shape what senior managers aim for and how they act.
    • From the bottom: who the company hires, and what new staff learn from coworkers.
    • Both ways: once a culture exists, it also shapes how managers and staff behave.
    • Results: satisfaction, commitment and performance.

    中文邏輯文化不是主管宣布就成立：上面要有價值與行動，下面要「選對人」和「同事帶新人」，兩邊都做到才穩。

    3. Six elements: the toolkit

    Slide p.8 lists six elements under the title “This is how to create a corporate culture.” So they are tools you can use, not just things to notice.

    The easiest way to tell them apart is to ask what form each one takes: a principle, a person, an object, an activity or a story.

    ElementWhat it isAsk yourselfExample
    1. Values 核心價值A principleDoes it say how we should act?“Customer first”; the RESPECT value set (p.11)
    2. Heroes 英雄模範A personIs there someone people copy as “the right way to work”?Prince Mahidol; Bill Gates
    3. Traditions and symbolsAn object that lastsCan you see or hear it, like a song, logo or poem?Company song or poem
    4. Rites and rituals 儀式An activity that is not about praiseDoes someone change status (rite)? Or is it a routine everyone repeats (ritual)?Induction, promotion, retirement; weekly meetings, yearly reviews
    5. CeremoniesAn activity to praise peopleIs the main point to honor someone in public?Awards night
    6. StoriesSomething that happened, retoldDoes it have a person, an event and a result that people repeat?Founder stories; gossip

    Values: the center

    Core values and commitments+Norms of behavior→Healthy culture→Effective organization

    Values alone are not enough. They need matching everyday behavior (norms). Slide p.10 gives five signs that a value is real and not just a poster:

    1. VisionIt appears in the company vision
    2. LeadersLeaders act on it
    3. DecisionsIt is used to make decisions
    4. ViolationBreaking it gets a strong reaction
    5. RewardPeople are rewarded for living it

    Heroes: values in a person

    Heroes show what success looks like in real life. They are role models. They stand for what the company believes, and they set the standard others aim for (p.12).

    Mahidol University: Prince Mahidol

    Stands for service to society, knowledge built on research, and serving everyone, not only the elite (Kantabutra and Saratun, 2013).

    Microsoft: Bill Gates

    Stands for hard work, persistence and competition: do what it takes to get the job done and win.

    The two heroes stand for very different things. Mahidol stands for service; Gates stands for winning. Each one makes his organization's values easy to picture.

    Shared events: rites, rituals and ceremonies

    Shared events carry the culture and show people “the way we do things around here” (p.16). Three questions tell the types apart:

    AskIf yes, it is aWho goes through itExample
    Does someone change status?Rite of passageEveryone who reaches that point, however well they performInduction, promotion, retirement
    Is it a routine everyone repeats, with no star?RitualEveryone, again and againThe weekly meeting format, yearly reviews, retreats
    Is the main point to honor people in public?CeremonyOnly the people chosen for itTeam of the year awards

    Short version: everyone goes through a rite, everyone repeats a ritual, and only standout people are honored at a ceremony.

    Promotion shows the difference. The day someone becomes a manager is a rite. The quarterly managers' review meeting is a ritual. The “manager of the year” award is a ceremony.

    A rite of passage does three things for a newcomer (p.17): it turns an outsider into an insider, gives a sense of belonging, and shows “how we do things around here.” A tradition or symbol is different again: it is an object, such as a company song, that lasts and keeps the culture stable.

    The slide lists retirement as a rite. A farewell party that thanks the person in public also works as a ceremony.

    Stories

    Stories, gossip and the grapevine all carry messages about what the company values (p.18). The slides give two examples. One is a quote from Prince Mahidol: “True success is not in learning but in its application for the benefit of mankind” (p.19). The other is Saha Group's story about its founder, shown with its values: social responsibility, advancement, harmony, passion for excellence and truthfulness (p.20).

    A value on its own is abstract. A story shows the value in action. People pass stories around anyway, so managers should pay attention to which stories are spreading.

    Pairs people mix up

    PairHow to tell them apart
    Values / storiesA value is the rule, such as “customer first.” A story is an event that proves it, such as the founder driving all night to deliver an urgent order.
    Heroes / storiesThe person is the hero. What people say about what that person did is the story. The same founder can be both.
    Symbols / ritualsThe company song is a symbol. Singing it at the start of every meeting is a ritual.
    Values / visionA vision says where the company is going. Values say how people should act on the way.
    Ceremonies / rewardsA reward is money or a prize for one person. A ceremony is the public event where everyone sees it given.

    判斷順序先問形式：一句原則、一個人、一個東西、一個活動，還是一段經過。是活動的話，再問有沒有人換身分、是不是固定重複、是不是為了表揚。

    4. Culture, performance and change

    Kotter and Heskett (1992), p.21What it means for a manager
    Culture has a big effect on long-term financial results.Culture affects results, so it is not just a “soft” issue.
    The effect is even bigger when things change fast.During a turnaround, culture can decide whether the change works.
    Cultures that hold back growth are common and form easily.If no one shapes the culture, it tends to go in the wrong direction.
    Culture is hard to change, but it can be reshaped.Change is possible, but it is slow and has to be planned.

    In a weak culture, people are not lazy. They are pushing in different directions. Vision, values and strategy give everyone the same direction. Leadership then gets everyone moving that way.

    Culture changes more easily when (p.23)

    A crisis threatens the company's survival. A new leader takes over with backing from the top, such as the board. The company is small and young. Or the culture is weak.

    Even then

    Changing a culture takes years. Strong cultures are harder to change than weak ones. That helps when the culture is good and hurts when it needs to change.

    Learning objective 3 mentions culture “health and strength,” but the slides do not define strength. A common way to read it: strength is how many people share the culture; health is whether the culture helps the company reach its goals. A culture can be strong and still unhealthy.

    5. Building an ethical culture

    Ethics: “standards of conduct that indicate how one should behave based on moral duties and virtues arising from principles about right and wrong.” (Josephson, 1990)

    In plain words, ethics is a standard for how people should act, based on what is right and wrong. Slide p.27 shows the six elements again, with an arrow labeled “Ethical value” pointing to Values. So you build an ethical culture with the same six tools. You just put ethics into the core values.

    What managers can do (p.15)Element it usesExample
    Make ethics a core valueValuesList integrity as a value and describe what it looks like at work
    Be a visible role modelHeroesA leader turns down a profitable shortcut that is unfair, in front of the team
    Communicate what is expectedRitualsRepeat the expectations at regular reviews
    Provide ethics trainingRitesEvery induction includes an ethics session
    Reward ethical acts and punish unethical ones, openlyCeremonies; a strong reaction when values are brokenThank someone in public for raising a safety concern
    Provide ways to protect people who speak upThe systems behind the cultureA safe channel to report wrongdoing
    DiscretionaryBe a good corporate citizen; give back to the community
    EthicalDo what is right, just and fair; avoid harm
    LegalObey the law; play by the rules
    EconomicBe profitable: everything else rests on this

    Read the pyramid from the bottom. A company has to make money first, or it cannot do anything else for long. Above that, it must obey the law. Being ethical goes further than the law: something can be legal and still wrong. Giving back to the community is the top layer, and it is voluntary.

    The pyramid comes from slide p.29, which cites the World Business Council for Sustainable Development. Most textbooks call it Carroll's pyramid of corporate social responsibility.

    Stakeholders (p.28): how well a company is managed affects four groups: the market (shareholders, customers, analysts), the workplace (employees, unions), the community (local people) and the environment (government, NGOs).

    Slide p.25 gives Citibank, Wal-Mart and Chevron as examples of unethical behavior. Use them as class examples only. The slide gives no sources or dates.

    容易混淆Legal 是法律規定的最低標準；Ethical 是法律沒要求但應該做對的事。合法不等於合乎倫理。

    Integrity in action

    Integrity is the value most likely to stay on paper. So it needs the most concrete behaviors. For a medical supplier, it means:

    • Tell customers the truth about stock, delivery dates and product limits.
    • Never sell expired, damaged or wrongly stored products.
    • No gifts or favors to win hospital orders.
    • Report mistakes early, including your own.
    • Speak up when something feels wrong.

    8. Do not mix these up

    ConceptsFirstSecond
    Culture / structureCulture is the unwritten way people work.Structure is the formal chart of who reports to whom.
    Corporate / national cultureCorporate culture belongs to one company.National culture (Session 6) is an average for a whole country.
    Vision / valuesA vision says where the company is going.Values say how people should act on the way.
    Rite / ritualA rite marks a change in one person's status.A ritual is a routine everyone repeats.
    Ritual / ceremonyA ritual is part of normal work.A ceremony is a special event to honor people.

    9. Cold call practice

    What is corporate culture?

    “The way we do things around here” (Deal and Kennedy, 1982). It is the shared values and habits that guide people, even when no one is supervising them.

    Name the five functions of culture.

    It gives the company an identity, gives work meaning, sets standards, helps people make sense of events, and guides behavior without supervision.

    How does culture form?

    From the top, through the founders' beliefs and what senior managers do. From the bottom, through who is hired and what they learn from coworkers. Culture then affects satisfaction, commitment and performance.

    What are the six elements?

    Values, heroes, traditions and symbols, rites and rituals, ceremonies, and stories.

    Why are values the center?

    Values plus everyday behavior make a healthy culture, and a healthy culture makes an effective organization. Leaders have to act on values, use them, defend them and reward them.

    What does a rite of passage do?

    It turns a newcomer into an insider, gives them a sense of belonging and shows them how things are done.

    How does culture affect performance?

    It has a big effect on long-term results, especially when things change fast. Bad cultures form easily. Culture is hard to change, but it can be reshaped.

    When is culture easier to change?

    After a crisis, when a new leader has backing from the top, in small young companies, or when the culture is weak. It still takes years.

    How do you build an ethical culture?

    Make ethics a core value. Then model it, communicate it, train people, reward and punish openly, and protect people who speak up.

    What are the four corporate responsibilities?

    Economic (the base), legal, ethical and discretionary.
