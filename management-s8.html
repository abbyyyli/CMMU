    1. What culture is and what it does

    “Culture is the way we do things around here, or what keeps the herd moving in roughly the right direction.” (Deal and Kennedy, 1982)

    The definition has two halves. Culture is how people actually behave, and it steers a group without a fence. “Roughly” matters: culture guides direction; it does not dictate every step. The deck opens with a related idea: sustainability culture, the beliefs, norms and practices tied to an organization's environmental impact, matters most during transitions, when practices are changing (Assoratgoon and Kantabutra, 2023).

    Function (p.5)Plain meaningQuick example
    Creates corporate identityWho “we” are, for staff and customersStaff and customers describe the firm the same way
    Creates meaning and commitmentWhy the work mattersPeople stay because they believe in the purpose
    Identifies expectations and standardsWhat good work looks likeNew staff learn the quality bar from peers
    A tool for sense-makingHow to read what happensAfter a reorganization, people know how to interpret it
    Shapes behavior without direct supervisionWhat people do when nobody checksA night-shift worker fixes a customer issue unasked

    Culture is informal control. Slide p.6 calls it a non-formal way of controlling behavior: values and norms shape daily practice, and unwritten rules pass from staff to staff. Public and private organizations both have cultures.

    Structure (Session 7)

    Formal control: who reports to whom and who may talk to whom.

    Culture (Session 8)

    Informal control: what people do because “that is how we work here.”

    容易混淆S6 的 national culture 是一個國家的平均傾向；這堂的 corporate culture 是一家公司的做事方式。兩者都是共享、學來的，但層次不同。

    2. How culture forms

    • From the top: founders' philosophy and values shape senior management's mission and goals, and management's strategy and actions.
    • From the bottom: who is hired (selection) and how newcomers learn from peers (socialization).
    • Two-way: once culture forms, it shapes what managers and staff do next.
    • Outcomes: satisfaction, commitment and performance.

    中文邏輯文化不是主管宣布就成立：上面要有價值與行動，下面要「選對人」和「同事帶新人」，兩邊都做到才穩。

    Heroes: values in a person

    Heroes make success real, attainable and human; provide role models; symbolize what the company stands for; preserve what makes it special; set the performance standard; and motivate employees (p.12).

    Mahidol University: Prince Mahidol

    Service to society; knowledge based on research; serving all levels of society, not only the elite (Kantabutra and Saratun, 2013).

    Microsoft: Bill Gates

    Hard working; persistent; competitive; do what it takes to get the job done and win.

    Heroes can carry very different values. What matters is that the company's own values become a person people can picture.

    Stories

    Stories, the grapevine, rumor and gossip all carry key messages (p.18). The slides show a Prince Mahidol quote, “True success is not in learning but in its application for the benefit of mankind” (p.19), and Saha Group's founder story alongside values such as social responsibility, advancement, harmony, passion for excellence and truthfulness (p.20).

    A value is abstract; a story shows it in action with a person, a problem and a result. The grapevine spreads stories whether managers like it or not, so the practical question is which stories are spreading.

    容易混淆Rite 是某個人的轉折點（入職、升遷、退休）；Ritual 是大家固定的做事方式；Ceremony 是表揚人的大型活動。

    4. Culture, performance and change

    Kotter and Heskett (1992), p.21What it means for a manager
    Culture can significantly affect long-term economic performance.Culture is a performance lever, not a soft extra.
    It matters even more in times of rapid change.Turnarounds and transitions are when culture decides success.
    Cultures that impede financial growth are common and develop easily.An unshaped culture tends to drift the wrong way.
    Cultures are hard to change but can be reshaped.Change is possible, deliberate and slow.

    A weak culture is not people being lazy; it is people working in different directions. Vision, values and strategy supply a direction; leadership gets everyone aligned with it.

    Culture is more likely to change when (p.23)

    A dramatic crisis threatens survival; leadership turnover is backed by the top, such as the board; the company is small and young; or the culture is weak.

    Even then

    Cultural change takes years. Strong cultures change less easily than weak ones, so strength cuts both ways.

    Learning objective 3 asks about culture “health and strength.” The slides label weak, better and healthy cultures but do not define strength separately. A common reading: strength is how widely the culture is shared; health is whether it supports the organization's goals.

    5. Building an ethical culture

    Ethics: “standards of conduct that indicate how one should behave based on moral duties and virtues arising from principles about right and wrong.” (Josephson, 1990)

    Slide p.27 repeats the six elements with an “Ethical value” arrow pointing at Values. An ethical culture uses the same toolkit, with ethics placed among the core values.

    Pyramid from slide p.29 (source printed: World Business Council for Sustainable Development). It is widely known as Carroll's pyramid of corporate social responsibility.

    Stakeholders (p.28): management quality affects the marketplace (shareholders, customers, financial analysts), the workplace (employees, unions), the community (local communities) and the environment (government, NGOs).

    Slide p.25 lists Citibank, Wal-Mart and Chevron as examples of unethical behavior. Treat them as course illustrations; the slide gives no sources or dates.

    60 to 90 second presentation answer
    “LMS has made real progress: a development plan, a shared vision, quarterly feedback, team rewards and executive coaching, and high-flyers have come back while innovation has recovered. Apichart's concern is that this depends on him. Using the six elements of culture, LMS has a vision but no stated core values, few heroes besides Apichart, and no rituals or ceremonies. I would first create three to five core values with employees, such as customer first, learning, respect and ownership of results, and make leaders model and reward them. Then I would build three shared events: a Welcome to LMS induction as a rite of passage, a quarterly Customer and Ideas Day as a ritual, and an annual Values Day as a celebration. I would also hire and appraise on these values, develop a successor, and make integrity a core value. Culture change takes years, so I would track values understanding, retention, customer results and new products every year.”

    8. Do not mix these up

    ConceptsFirstSecond
    Culture / structureCulture is informal control: how we do things.Structure is formal control: who reports to whom.
    Corporate / national cultureCorporate culture belongs to one organization.National culture (Session 6) is a country-level average.
    Vision / valuesA vision says where the organization is going.Values say how people should behave on the way.
    Rite / ritualA rite marks one person's key moment.A ritual is a routine way of working.
    Ritual / ceremonyA ritual repeats as part of work.A ceremony honors people at a major event.
    Strong / healthyStrength is how widely a culture is shared.Health is whether it supports the goals; a strong culture can be unhealthy.
    Legal / ethicalLegal is what the law requires.Ethical is what is right, just and fair, beyond the law.
    Reward / ceremonyA reward is paid to a person.A ceremony celebrates it in front of others and carries the value.

    9. Cold call practice

    What is corporate culture?

    “The way we do things around here” (Deal and Kennedy, 1982): shared values and norms that guide behavior, even without supervision.

    Name the five functions of culture.

    Identity; meaning and commitment; performance expectations and standards; sense-making; shaping behavior without direct control.

    How does culture form?

    From founders' values, senior management's mission and actions, and from selection and socialization of staff. Culture then shapes satisfaction, commitment and performance.

    What are the six elements?

    Values, heroes and heroines, traditions and symbols, rites and rituals, ceremonies, and stories.

    Why are values the core?

    Core values with norms of behavior create a healthy culture, which creates an effective organization. Leaders must model, use, defend and reward them.

    What does a rite of passage do?

    It turns outsiders into insiders, creates belonging and teaches newcomers how things are done.

    How does culture affect performance?

    It strongly affects long-term performance, even more in rapid change. Harmful cultures form easily; culture is hard to change but can be reshaped.

    When is culture more likely to change?

    After a crisis, with leadership turnover backed by the top, in small young firms, or when the culture is weak. It still takes years.

    How do you build an ethical culture?

    Make ethics a core value, model it, communicate it, train it, visibly reward and punish, and provide protective mechanisms.

    What are the four corporate responsibilities?

    Economic (the foundation), legal, ethical and discretionary.
