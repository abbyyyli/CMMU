    Strategic Marketing Management · Session 8

    New Products, Pricing and Grey Markets

    Most new products fail because buyers see no real benefit, and most price cuts backfire because they teach buyers to watch price instead of the brand. This session covers where ideas come from, why tests mislead, how to set and signal a price, and why price gaps between markets invite grey imports.

    Chapter map

    Real benefit→Test it honestly→Price that fits the position→Price psychology→Avoid discount traps→Grey markets

    Key idea

    A new product needs a benefit people will pay for, not just a new form. Price is the only part of the marketing mix that brings in money, so it must match the brand's position and stay consistent.

    Based on the Session 8 slides and lecture, with questions from the lecturer's two discussion videos added to the practice items. Prices and figures are the lecturer's classroom examples, not verified market data.

    1. Why most new products fail

    Key idea

    Huge amounts are spent on new products, yet most fail. The lecturer's main reason is simple: a lack of real benefits. The product is not very new, and buyers do not see a need for it.

    Part of a conceptMeaningExampleDo not confuse with
    FormWhat the product actually isToothpaste as a paste, a powder or a liquidA new benefit
    TechnologyHow it is made; can we do it?A foldable screenA reason to buy
    Benefit 利益Something buyers want and will pay forShaving cream as a paper sheet you can carry on a flight or a camping tripNovelty that works only once

    How it works

    Changing the form is the easiest way to make something look new, so ask "why?" each time. A new form can also change the benefit, for better or worse. Whatever you make will probably be copied quickly. Being too different is a risk too, because people resist change at first.

    中文邏輯形式、技術、利益三者裡，最常缺的是利益。換個形式不等於多了好處，要一直問「為什麼要買」。

    2. Where new product ideas come from

    Key idea

    Start close to the customer. People may not know what engineers can build, but pain points show what is worth building. Grab spread fast because it solved a real problem with taxis.

    SourceWhat it revealsClass example
    Complaints and repair logsWhat keeps going wrongMany repairs point to a needed improvement
    EmployeesDaily problems outsiders missToyota is known for using staff suggestions
    SalespeopleWhat customers and rivals sayThey meet both, and people talk to them freely
    Watching customersReal behavior before formal researchA film producer who listens to audience reactions in cinemas
    CompetitorsIdeas to copy or improveWatch launches and ads; reverse-engineer

    Class example · the problem list

    List the problems people have with a product, how often and how serious they are, then look for clusters. The telephone story shows both the method and its trap:

    Rotary phone→Push-button→Hand tied up, wired to the wall→Cordless phone→"I lost my phone"→Idea: connect it to the wall

    Ideas can drift into "stupid territory", like a paid service to remember where you parked. Test every idea with one question: would people pay for this?

    Gap map 缺口圖

    A perceptual map can show empty positions. On a "crunch" axis, apples sit next to potato chips; on "healthy crunch", apple chips win. But a gap is not automatically an opportunity: think of cheap luxury cars.

    Product Innovation Charter (PIC)

    Before investing, check the fit: do we have the skills and technology, do we know these customers, what are cost and competition, and how long before others copy us? Then size the market, profit and rivals.

    3. New benefit, or just a new form?

    Key idea

    Practice · Real benefit or just a new form?

    Class example · detergent

    Powder could turn hard in a wet or humid yard. Liquid is easier but heavy, and "you're paying me for the water". Tablets are tidy, yet people stop over-pouring because nobody breaks a tablet in half, so the seller loses volume. Pods combine detergent, softener and color care: convenient, expensive, and quickly copied by every brand.

    Type of new productMeaningExample

    4. Testing new products, and why tests mislead

    Idea generation→Screening→Concept development and testing→Business analysis→Prototype→Test marketing→Commercialization

    Key idea

    Concept testing checks the biggest cause of failure early: the buyer did not see a need. It saves money and sets the positioning, but no test is 100% sure.

    Too new: people say no

    People resist change and cannot imagine a microwave, fax or video call before using one. Computers, fax machines and VCRs were rejected at first. Do not kill a radical idea too quickly.

    Not new: people say yes

    Politeness and over-claiming push stated intent up: "Would you buy it? Yes", then they don't. The honeymoon effect gives high early scores that fade. New Coke and Cherry Coke scored well and failed.

    How it works

    The discussion video asks why the most successful new products were often kept away from the formal NPD system. Its own answer: formal systems are routine and slow, big committees lose a shared team identity, and they judge new ideas by old criteria, which favors more of the same.

    中文邏輯測試兩頭都會錯：太新的東西大家先說不要，不新的東西大家客氣說要。所以要看實際的重複購買，而不是嘴上說的意願。

    5. Price: the only P that brings in money

    Key idea

    Product, place and promotion are costs; price is the only P that generates revenue. It must link to positioning and stay consistent, because switching between premium and value makes a brand look like a yo-yo. "Me too but cheaper" means everyone makes less money.

    Pricing objectiveMeaningLecture point
    SurvivalCope with fierce competition or excess capacity"Not good at all"
    Current profit maximizationChoose the price that gives the most profit nowDo not say it out loud; a Thai firm named "The Profitable Group" invites the question "profit from whom?"
    Market-share leadershipLargest share, often through the lowest priceDiscounts can buy share, but not profit or loyalty
    Product-quality leadershipHigher price, higher quality, more R&D"Expensive, so it must be good" is learned, not a law

    How it works · buying share is not buying loyalty

    Many firms enter cheap, planning to raise prices once customers are "loyal". When the price rises, people move to the next cheap brand. Blank CDs at 4–5 baht kept creeping up, and buyers left. Oppo entered around 4,000 baht against a 25,000-baht iPhone and has had to launch higher models to build reputation.

    Phone brandPricing pathLesson
    AppleAlways premiumA consistent price protects the image
    OppoLow, then trading upShare first, credibility later
    iMobilePhones made by others under a local nameWorks like a private label
    SamsungEntered Thai phones high, despite a cheap imageA high price plus real features shifted the whole brand upward

    6. Price signals and reference prices

    Key idea

    Price signaling 價格訊號: a high price suggests high quality, a low price suggests "must be pretty bad". A reference price 參考價格 is the rough price in a buyer's head used to judge every brand in a category.

    Class example · Thai green tea

    For years most people said a bottle of green tea costs 20 baht, because Oishi and Unif priced there. Unif's Haru-cha at 35 baht, sold as first-harvest leaves, failed. The lecturer heard that the tea costs about 2 baht to produce, before transport, distribution and marketing.

    Decision lab · Price a new green tea

    The shelf already has famous brands at a 20-baht reference price. Choose a launch price and see what the shopper is likely to think.

    Retail pricingPatternExamples
    EDLP (everyday low price)Cheap all the time; a volume modelBig C, Tesco Lotus, Walmart
    High-LowHigh every day, with some promotionsVilla Market
    Private label 自有品牌Usually 20–30% cheaper than national brands; in Thailand often only about 10%Tops' My Choice and Smarter

    How it works

    In surveys people say price is "extremely important", yet asked right after an item goes into the cart, most cannot say what they paid or whether it was on sale. "Customers don't care about price" sounds good but can be bad news: it may mean they see all brands as the same and will drift to private label.

    7. Setting the price: floor, ceiling and strategy

    Floor = production cost→Price somewhere between→Ceiling = consumer perception→Capped in practice by competition

    How it works

    Textbook models say nobody would pay 500 baht for water. The lecturer disagrees: some people always buy the most expensive brand, applied unevenly across categories. What really caps the price is that "you're not usually alone in the market": Intel faces AMD, and Oculus leans on its game library, because hardware without games is "a TV with no channels".

    ApproachDefinitionClass exampleDo not confuse with
    Cost-plusCost plus a standard markupA shoemaker's cost is 100 baht, sold at 110; retail is about 500What buyers would pay
    Value-based 價值定價What customers will pay; unrelated to costNike: about 200 baht to make, 5,000 to buyOvercharging (price gouging)
    Market skimming 吸脂定價High launch price for an innovation, falling laterIntel's top chip; PlayStation 3Permanent premium positioning
    Market penetration 滲透定價Low price, low margin, high volumeTwo Lancer pens for 5 baht; McDonald's in Western marketsA price war
    Unique or prestigious

    Buyers are less price sensitive.

    Hard to substitute

    Few alternatives, like gasoline for commuters.

    Small total cost

    The price barely matters to the buyer.

    Someone else pays

    Buyer, payer and user differ, as with a conference the university pays for.

    These four conditions make demand less price elastic. The lecturer's examples to test them: gasoline, cigarettes, eating out, movies and magazines.

    Practice · Name the pricing strategy

    Class example · Tata Nano

    Tata built "the cheapest car in the world" to replace families of five on one motorcycle. People said it was "a motorcycle with four wheels" and feared being judged as poor, so it did not sell. A low price can send a low-status signal.

    9. Price psychology

    Key idea

    The contrast principle 對比原則: when two things are compared and the second is quite different, people see the gap as bigger than it is. Start high and work down, and later items feel cheap.

    Class exampleWhat happens
    Suits at 18,000 and 34,000; add 28,000With two prices, shoppers stay on one side. A middle option lets 18,000 shoppers step up and 34,000 shoppers land at 28,000 instead of 18,000 (decoy pricing).
    Tailor shopsStart with imported Armani fabric, then step down; the cheapest level then looks poor.
    Accessories after a big purchaseShirts after a suit, a wallet after a handbag, a warranty after a laptop.
    Photocopiers A, B, CModel A, the most expensive, sold poorly; after a pricier model was added above it, A started selling.
    Odd pricing: 199, 6,999Buyers anchor on the first digit: "six something" is really seven.

    10. Price promotions and price cuts

    Key idea

    The more often a brand runs price promotions, the more shoppers focus on price, and everyone ends up trapped in promotions that cost money. The lecturer's advice: "stand tall", support the brand with marketing, and stay away from discounting unless you are clearing old stock.

    TermDefinitionClass example
    Loss leader 犧牲打An item sold below cost to bring shoppers into a storeCoke 1.25 L at 19 instead of about 35 baht; small-shop owners came back many times to resell it
    Forward buyingRetailers stock up during a deal, then stop orderingOften driven by year-end targets; can feed grey markets
    StockpilingConsumers buy extra during a dealFew do it; mostly educated, higher-income deal lovers. Nivea "25% off" was 40 to 30 baht
    Model-year discountOld models lose value when new ones arriveA logical reason to cut price
    1Low-quality trap

    Buyers question the product's quality.

    2Fragile market-share trap

    Price-sensitive buyers switch to the next cheaper rival.

    3Shallow-pockets trap

    Bigger rivals can last longer; a family firm with one brand against Unilever's hundreds. Fabric softener fell from 67 to 13 baht.

    How it works

    An unexplained cut makes buyers wonder whether the product has a problem, is being replaced, the company is in trouble, quality has dropped, or the price will fall further. Give a real reason, such as an anniversary or a share of proceeds for disaster relief, and do not promote so often that people learn to wait. When Chester's gave the lecturer 50% off on a Wednesday he never asked for, it raised the question: why full price on other days? Cutting prices to bankrupt rivals and then raising them is predatory pricing, which is illegal.

    11. Grey markets

    Key idea

    A grey market 灰色市場 (parallel import 平行輸入) is genuine branded goods bought in one market and resold in another outside authorized channels. It is unauthorized, but not illegal by definition, though tax or other laws may be broken in practice. The elderly market, once also called "grey", is now the silver market.

    1A big price gap

    The legitimate channel offers significant value to undercut.

    2A well-known product

    It sells itself, so no marketing is needed.

    3Price or currency differences

    Taxes, refunds or exchange rates create the margin.

    Class caseWhat happened
    Marlboro (slide, 2025)About $4 a pack in Thailand vs $21.6 in the UK. "Would you not feel some temptation?" The same logic applies to SCG cement within ASEAN.
    Video games at MBKGrey sellers let buyers play today, instead of waiting, paying shipping and tax online.
    VietnamShoppers buy Thai-made Colgate, Pantene and Coke over locally made versions of the same brands.
    Carrefour perfumeSold 50% below normal price; people did not buy because they doubted it was real.
    Tesco and a jeans brandThe brand refused to supply a low-image store; Tesco sourced the jeans abroad, and in court the supplier was revealed and blacklisted. The lecturer named Tommy Hilfiger; the well-known court case was Levi's.
    Johnnie WalkerBans discounting; a Thai retailer caught discounting got no stock for six months.
    Grey-market carsMercedes may refuse service; Toyota services them at 15% more, which the lecturer calls "pretty clever".
    Pros

    Lower prices, access to models not sold locally, and more coverage for the brand, which still sells its product.

    Cons

    Image damage, warranty disputes, conflict with official distributors, and broken price discipline. Buyers often do not know an item is grey market, which is not their fault.

    Any price difference between areas, such as zone pricing, opens the door to grey markets.

    12. Do not mix these up

    Keep separateReason
    New form / new benefitLiquid toothpaste changes the form, not what it does for you.
    Stated intent / real buyingPoliteness and the honeymoon effect inflate test scores.
    Market share / profitShare bought with discounts is not loyal and may lose money.
    High price / high qualityA learned signal, not a law.
    Cost-plus / value-basedCost plus markup versus what customers will pay.
    Optional / captive productYou may add an accessory; you must buy a captive refill.
    Forward buying / stockpilingRetailers stocking up versus consumers stocking up.
    Grey market / counterfeitGenuine goods through unauthorized channels versus fakes.

    13. Cold call practice

    Why do most new products fail?

    Mostly because buyers do not see a need: the product is not very new and offers no real benefit beyond a new form.

    Should new products be technology-driven or market-driven?

    Start from the customer's pain point. Technology answers "can we?", while the market answers "should we?". Grab and online banking spread quickly because they solved real problems.

    Why are test-market results usually better than results after launch?

    Novelty and the honeymoon effect, extra support in test areas, competitors not yet reacting, and politeness inflating stated intent.

    Why is price the most important P for revenue?

    It is the only part of the mix that generates revenue; product, place and promotion are costs.

    What is a reference price?

    The rough price in a buyer's head for a category, used to judge every brand. For bottled green tea in Thailand, it was about 20 baht.

    What is price signaling?

    Using price as a quality cue: a high price suggests high quality and a low price suggests low quality, which private labels suffer from.

    Skimming or penetration: what is the difference?

    Skimming launches an innovation at a high price that falls later; penetration sets a low price for high volume and share.

    Why did the Tata Nano fail?

    Its very low price signaled low status; people saw it as "a motorcycle with four wheels" and feared being judged as poor.

    What is captive product pricing?

    Selling the main product cheaply and earning the margin on items that must be used with it, such as razor blades or printer ink.

    What is the contrast principle?

    When the second item compared is quite different from the first, the gap seems bigger than it is, so starting high makes later items feel cheaper.

    Why are price promotions dangerous?

    They train shoppers to focus on price and wait for sales, lower margins, and can trigger the low-quality, fragile-share and shallow-pockets traps.

    What is a grey market, and when is it most likely?

    Genuine goods resold across markets outside authorized channels. It is likely when the price gap is large, the product is well known and sells itself, and prices or currencies differ.

    14. Answer templates

    Evaluate a new product

    Changing the form from [A] to [B] may look new, but the question is what benefit it adds. Unless customers see a need, it will mostly win one-time trial. I would test repeat purchase, not just stated intent.

    Recommend a launch price

    Shoppers judge this category against a reference price of about [X]. A lower price may simply look cheap and is hard to raise later, so I would price at [level] because [benefit or positioning], and state any introductory discount clearly.

    Advise on a price cut

    I would avoid cutting the price because buyers may read it as [problem or lower quality], price-sensitive buyers will switch again, and [rival] can outlast us. If we must, I would give a clear reason, limit how often we do it, and compare the extra volume with the lost margin.

    Explain a grey market

    This is a grey market: genuine products moving between markets outside authorized channels because of a price gap. It widens reach, but it can hurt image and cause warranty and distributor conflicts. I would [tolerate, service at a premium or narrow the price gap] depending on [brand and channel impact].
