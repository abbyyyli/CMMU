    CRM, Customer Data and Marketing Metrics

    More data is not better insight. Turn customer interactions into useful information, use it to improve relationships and experience, and test whether actions change behavior and create economic value.

    1. From transaction to relationship

    Transaction 單次交易

    How do I make this sale?

    Relationship 關係行銷

    How do I create enough value that this customer keeps choosing us? "Buy one time vs a lifetime."

    CRM 顧客關係管理 is managing acquisition, retention and value creation over time. It is not merely software.

    Customer lifetime value

    CLV is the economic value of a relationship over time, not today's revenue. It depends on frequency, retention, margin, serving cost and acquisition cost. The professor's warning: 100% loyalty is unrealistic.

    An expensive customer to acquire is worth it only if the relationship lasts long enough to repay the cost. Retention is not the goal for every customer at any cost.

    2. Satisfaction, importance and experience

    The lowest satisfaction score is not automatically the top priority. Weight it by how much the attribute matters.

    AttributeSatisfactionImportancePriority
    Store music5/102/10Low, despite the lower score
    Coffee taste7/1010/10Examine first

    Queue time and Wi-Fi are added examples. The quadrant view is a standard importance-performance supplement.

    Hygiene versus motivators

    Must-have (hygiene)

    Correct order, cleanliness, working payment, no defects. Failure creates dissatisfaction; success only meets expectations.

    Nice-to-have (motivator)

    Personal greeting, remembering the usual order, a surprise. Adds delight but cannot replace the basics.

    Fix serious hygiene failures first, then add experience enhancers. Customer experience management means learning fast enough to respond while the problem still matters.

    DataObservations.

    MIS and garbage in, garbage out

    A management information system turns data into information for decisions. Technology cannot fix wrong data, the wrong question or poor interpretation.

    Information overload

    Ask what decision we are making, which information is relevant, which metric would change the decision, and what can be ignored.

    4. Data mining and evidence discipline

    Data mining: computers filter large datasets for patterns; humans interpret. It shows what happened better than why.

    Large dataset→Pattern→Human interpretation→Hypothesis, not conclusion
    Supported

    Morning purchases declined.

    Not yet supported

    Customers left because prices are too high. Competitors, routines, weather, stock-outs, location or service could explain it.

    CaseWhat data does wellLimit
    ZaraActual store sales drive production instead of forecasts alone.A pattern in Store A may not hold in Store B, next year or another country.
    7-ElevenMorning patterns differ by school versus factory locations.Local data supports local decisions; do not generalize too quickly.
    Behavioral dataShows what customers actually did: purchases, searches, clicks, visits.A running-shoe search could be a gift, research or a school project. Behavior is not motive.

    Investigation versus intervention

    Investigation 調查原因

    Why is this happening? Compare segments and stores, collect feedback.

    Intervention 介入測試

    If we do X, will behavior change? Run a targeted test and measure it.

    Pattern→Investigate cause→Hypothesis→Intervene→Measure behavior

    Jumping straight to intervention can fix the wrong problem and makes results hard to interpret. Session 3 covers how to test causality.

    5. Prediction, privacy and trust

    Prediction is not certainty

    AI and predictive models estimate patterns from historical data. New competitors, technology, shocks or changing preferences can break them. Past behavior is evidence, not destiny.

    Personalization versus privacy

    The more data is linked to a person, the more valuable and the more sensitive it becomes. Too much relevance turns into "how do they know that?"

    Do not ask how much data we can collect. Ask what data is necessary to improve the customer experience or the decision.

    6. Omnichannel and e-commerce

    Multichannel

    Store, website, app and social media, possibly run separately.

    Omnichannel 全通路

    Channels integrated into one seamless journey: research on mobile, check stock online, buy in store, return elsewhere.

    More channels mean more cost, complexity and consistency problems. Ask which channels matter to the target and what role each plays.

    E-commerce is not "just build a website"

    Traffic

    "If you build it, they will come": probably not. The funnel still applies.

    Trust

    Customers cannot touch the product: show company, security, price, delivery and guarantees.

    Logistics

    Inventory, delivery, fulfillment and returns remain physical.

    Revenue model

    Who pays, for what, how often? Users are not revenue.

    Pure play is fully online; clicks-and-mortar combines both. Do not ask which is modern; ask which creates better value and economics here. E-commerce is a trade-off, not an automatically superior channel.

    8. Loyalty programs

    A loyalty program is an intervention, not the objective. Membership is not behavioral loyalty, and behavioral loyalty is not attitudinal loyalty.

    Program value ≈ incremental customer value − reward and program cost

    Ask the counterfactual 反事實: what would this customer have done without the program? Rewarding behavior that would have happened anyway only reduces margin.

    9. Marketing metrics

    The professor: "How should we keep score?" First answer: "What are our objectives?" Measurable does not mean useful.

    Right order

    Objective → desired behavior → metric.

    Wrong order

    Available metric → pretend it is the objective.

    Impression→Click→Visit→Purchase→Repeat→Profit

    CTR is an intermediate metric. Likes and views become vanity metrics 虛榮指標 when they are not tied to the objective. Even sales can mislead: the market grew, prices rose, discounts cut margin, or acquisition rose while retention collapsed.

    11. Do not mix these up

    CRM = software.

    CRM is relationship management; tools support it.

    Acquiring customers = growing.

    Subtract churn.

    Lowest satisfaction = top priority.

    Weight by importance.

    Pattern = cause.

    Causal claims need stronger evidence.

    Investigation = intervention.

    Diagnosing is not testing a solution.

    AI prediction = certainty.

    Historical patterns can break.

    Omnichannel = every platform.

    Integration matters, not channel count.

    E-commerce = no logistics.

    Fulfillment and returns remain.

    Membership = loyalty.

    Look for incremental behavior.

    High CTR or likes = success.

    Define the objective first.

    12. Cold call practice

    We acquired 1,000 customers and lost 900. What does that tell you?

    Net growth is only 100. Acquisition alone hides a retention problem.

    Why can small repeated purchases beat one large purchase?

    Lifetime value depends on frequency, retention, margin and acquisition cost, not today's ticket.

    Why might coffee taste come before store music?

    Taste is far more important to the customer's decision, even with a higher satisfaction score.

    Why can't falling purchases prove prices are too high?

    The pattern shows what changed, not why. Other explanations remain.

    Investigation versus intervention?

    Investigation looks for the cause; intervention tests whether an action changes behavior.

    When does personalization backfire?

    When tracking feels intrusive and reduces trust.

    Why is having many channels not omnichannel?

    Channels need consistent information and an integrated experience.

    Why doesn't a large user base mean a viable business?

    Users are not a revenue model; costs, monetization and retention decide viability.

    Why can rewards to existing frequent buyers destroy value?

    If behavior does not change, the firm pays rewards without incremental purchases.

    Why is high CTR not enough when the goal is repeat purchase?

    CTR measures an earlier step, not repeat behavior or its economic value.

    Answer templates

    CRM problemObjective → customer → data → pattern → action → metric → risk (privacy, cost, irrelevance)
    Data questionEvidence → pattern → uncertainty → decision
    Digital businessCustomer value → friction → channel → trust → logistics → revenue model

    Data, channels and visible metrics are not the goal. The test is whether actions change meaningful customer behavior and create value without damaging margin or trust.
