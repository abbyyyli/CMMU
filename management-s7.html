    3. When communication breaks down

    Slide p.5 lists the consequences in this order:

    1. Lack of information
    2. Ambiguity, uncertainty
    3. Need for structure: people create new tasks, and effort is wasted
    4. Frustration, anger
    5. Feelings of isolation
    6. Inadequacy
    7. Withdrawal
    8. Poor performance, low morale

    The list starts with missing information and ends with poor performance. What looks like a people problem at the bottom may be an information problem at the top.

    The slide prints a list, not arrows between each item. Say “this can develop into,” not “A proves B.”

    Link to S4: withdrawal resembles Neglect in EVLN. The difference is the starting point: EVLN begins from dissatisfaction, while this list begins from missing information.

    Look at the system first

    6Organizations can create dropouts

    People seen as problems today may have been the brightest stars 10 years ago, but their talents were not used or developed.

    7Avoid blame

    Don't assume poor performance is an individual's fault. It can come from structure and process.

    8Effort at the bottom

    Effort must occur at the bottom to achieve the goals of the top, so the bottom needs information and support.

    Limit: the slides say poor performance can come from structure and process. They do not say individuals are never responsible.

    中文邏輯順序不要顛倒：先問結構和流程有沒有給他資訊與發揮空間，再問他個人有沒有問題。

    4. Five structures and the lattice

    Each structure answers one question: what are people grouped by? The grouping decides who talks to whom, and through whom.

    Functional 照專業分

    Grouped by
    Specialty: what people do.
    In the diagram
    CEO over Engineering, Marketing and Manufacturing (p.11).
    Communication question
    How do specialties coordinate on one product without sending everything up to the CEO?

    Product division 照產品分

    Grouped by
    Product: what people make or sell.
    In the diagram
    CEO over Product Divisions 1, 2 and 3 (p.11).
    Communication question
    How do divisions share knowledge instead of duplicating it?

    Matrix 縱橫交叉

    Grouped by
    Function and product at the same time.
    In the diagram
    Product Managers A to D report to the Director of Product Operations. Their lines cross every functional column and end at a customer (p.12).
    Communication question
    Who decides when a functional head and a product manager disagree?

    Horizontal 照流程分

    Grouped by
    End-to-end process, organized around teams.
    In the diagram
    A top management team; each process has a process owner and teams. New Product Development and Procurement and Logistics both end at the customer (p.13).
    Communication question
    How much can a team decide in front of the customer?

    Hybrid 功能＋流程

    Grouped by
    A functional band plus horizontal processes.
    In the diagram
    Ford Customer Service Division: Finance, Strategy and Communication, and Human Resources stay functional; three customer-facing groups run horizontally (p.15).
    Communication question
    Where is the boundary between the two parts, and who resolves issues across it?

    Lattice 人人直通

    Grouped by
    No fixed grouping: everyone is linked to everyone.
    In the diagram
    Vision emerges from the group; multi-skilled, self-managing members; no followers, and leaders may emerge; mutual sense making; buy in to shared values; anyone can reach anyone without an intermediary (p.9).
    Communication question
    With no intermediary, how does the group reach a decision?

    Lattice: shown on p.9, before the “Types of Organization Structure” divider, and not in the p.16 summary. Use it as the communication opposite of a hierarchy. “(control)” in its first bullet is not explained on the slide.

    Diagram sources on the slides: Nadler and Tushman (1988); Ostroff (1999); Byrne (1993); Stewart (1992); Ackerman (1982).

    容易混淆Product 與 Horizontal 都面向產出，但 Product 仍是 CEO 底下的事業部階層；Horizontal 依流程橫向排列、以團隊為單位、終點是顧客。

    5. Choosing a structure

    “There is no one best structure, depending on the requirements of each individual business.” (p.14)

    Situation (p.16 summary)StructureWatch for
    Small business, a few productsFunctionalAlso kept for any function needing full control
    Medium size, multiple productsMatrixShared specialists answer to function and product lines
    Really large, multiple groups of productsProduct divisionLarge is “really large” on the slide, not just “large”
    Frequent, direct customer contact, and the task does not require being correct 100% of the timeHorizontal (may be used)Both conditions are needed
    A function you want to keep under 100% controlFunctional, inside a hybridFord keeps Finance, Strategy and Communication, and HR functional

    Read the last two rules together. Teams close to the customer decide quickly, which suits work where a small mistake can be fixed next time. Where errors are not acceptable, keep that part under central, functional control.

    Limit: the slide gives no headcount or revenue for small, medium or really large. The Ford diagram fits the hybrid rule, but the slide does not state Ford's reasons.

    中文邏輯選結構看四件事：規模、產品數、與顧客接觸的頻率、需要多少控制。不是背哪個最好，而是說得出在什麼條件下用哪個。

    6. Communication networks simulation

    The second half of the session puts participants in a highly rigid, hierarchical organization: strict work rules, command and control, and top-down management and communication. They observe organizational productivity and their own feeling.

    1Examine how structure influences leadership, communication and decision-making.
    2Experience its impact on teamwork and productivity; morale, satisfaction and commitment; and personal effectiveness.
    3Build a framework for thinking about leadership and communication that helps people learn from experience.
    4Task: solve the problem as a team, before the other teams, within the time limit.

    Setup and rules

    Your organization (p.20)
    Directions (p.21)
    • Written messages only
    • No talking or non-verbal communication
    • Address every message, e.g. “To B”
    • Raise your hand; a postman delivers
    • Wrongly addressed mail goes to a lost mail bag
    • Tell the instructor by a note “To Instructor”
    • 30 minutes

    Trace a message

    Routes assume messages follow the lines on the chart. The supplied slides end at the directions, so the problem and the class debrief are not included here.

    What the setup predicts (reasoning from the rules, not reported results):

    • Every route passes through B. B is the bottleneck, and B's frame of reference filters everything.
    • Staff see only a fragment of the problem: lack of information, the first item on p.5.
    • Writing only removes tone and body language: mode of communication, on the Noise list.
    • Productivity and feeling mirror the two results on p.2: performance and satisfaction.
    • Link to S4: C, D and E have low autonomy, task identity and feedback, three JCM core dimensions.

    8. Cold call practice

    How do leaders achieve results?

    Through work culture, work process and people. Results are performance and satisfaction.

    Describe the linear model of communication.

    A speaker encodes and speaks a message, and listeners decode it. The arrow runs one way.

    What three factors influence communication?

    Context, the setting; frame of reference, what the listener brings; and noise, interference at the moment of delivery.

    What happens when communication breaks down?

    The slide runs from lack of information and uncertainty, through wasted effort, frustration, isolation and withdrawal, to poor performance and low morale.

    Is poor performance the individual's fault?

    Don't assume so. It can come from structure and process, so check the system before blaming the person.

    What is the best structure?

    There is no one best structure. Functional suits small firms with few products, matrix medium firms with multiple products, product division really large firms with product groups.

    When can a horizontal structure be used?

    Where customer contact is frequent and direct, and the task does not require being correct 100% of the time.

    What is a lattice organization?

    A network where everyone can reach everyone without an intermediary, vision comes from the group, and leaders emerge rather than being appointed.

    In the simulation, why does B matter so much?

    If messages follow the chart, every route between A and the staff, or between staff, passes through B.

    9. Answer templates

    Diagnose a performance problem

    “Before attributing this to the individual, I would check whether the structure gives ___ the information about ___. The case shows ___, which fits ___ on the breakdown list. The issue is ___.”

    Recommend a structure

    “Because the firm is ___ with ___ products, the summary points to a ___ structure. Functions that need full control, such as ___, can stay functional. I would track ___.”

    45-second model answer: “Our service unit performs badly. Should we replace the staff?”
    “Not before checking the system. The slides warn against assuming poor performance is an individual's fault, because it can come from structure and process. I would first ask what information staff receive, how many people a message passes through, and how much they may decide in front of a customer. If contact is frequent and small errors can be corrected, a horizontal, team-based design may fit. Only if performance stays poor after the information problems are fixed would I look at individual capability.”

    Source: Session 7 lecture slides, pp.2–22.
