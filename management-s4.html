    1. Job Characteristics Model

    Five job dimensions create three psychological states, which lead to work outcomes. Source: Robbins and Judge.

    DimensionMeaningDo not confuse with
    Skill variety 技能多樣性The job uses different skills and activitiesSimply adding more tasks, which can mean overload
    Task identity 任務完整性Completing a whole, identifiable piece of workTask significance (impact on others)
    Task significanceThe work affects other peopleAn important-sounding job title
    Autonomy 自主權Freedom in how the work is doneNo accountability or no standards
    FeedbackThe work itself shows how well it was doneHaving a KPI, which does not guarantee useful feedback

    Growth-need strength 成長需求強度 moderates the model: people who want challenge respond more strongly to enriched jobs. More autonomy is not a universal fix.

    2. Satisfaction, commitment and OCB

    Related, but not the same. The lecture links job satisfaction to citizenship, commitment, productivity, absenteeism and turnover. The arrows show relationships to examine, not proven causes.

    Job satisfaction

    "I like my job."

    Does not prove they will stay or perform well
    Organizational commitment 組織承諾

    "I believe in this organization and want to stay."

    Does not mean they like every task
    OCB 組織公民行為

    "I help a colleague even though no one pays me to."

    Does not follow from hitting sales targets

    Three components of commitment

    1. Strong acceptance of the organization's goals and values
    2. Willingness to put in considerable effort for the organization
    3. Desire to remain a member

    Organizational citizenship behavior (OCB) is discretionary, outside the formal reward system, and done without expecting a direct reward. A top performer can still show weak OCB.

    Motivation strategies of leaders

    Strategy on the slideLinks to
    Create and communicate the vision and every success behind itMeaning, recognition
    Participative managementTheory Y; participation in MBO
    Self-motivation through values alignment between individuals and the organizationSession 2 values and fit; commitment

    To analyze a leader, the slide asks: Theory X or Y? Intrinsic or extrinsic motivators? Which Maslow levels?

    3. Responses to dissatisfaction: EVLN

    Dissatisfied employees do not all quit. Two axes, active or passive and constructive or destructive, give four responses.

    注意Low morale 本身不能判斷員工會選哪一種反應，要看實際行為證據。投影片把 lateness / absence 放在 Exit；Robbins 課本放在 Neglect，考試照投影片寫，必要時向老師確認。

    The slide notes that exit, voice and loyalty apply to all stakeholder groups, not only employees.

    4. Reinforcement theory

    Consequences shape future behavior. A reward strengthens whatever behavior it actually pays for.

    Behavior→Consequence (reward or punishment)→Likelihood of repeating changes
    Positive reinforcement 正向增強

    Slide example: a bonus for reaching a sales quota, to encourage more sales effort.

    Punishment

    Slide example: a salary deduction for lateness, to encourage punctuality.

    TermAfter the behaviorAim
    Positive reinforcementAdd something desiredIncrease the behavior
    Negative reinforcementRemove something unpleasantIncrease the behavior
    PunishmentApply something unpleasantDecrease the behavior

    Negative reinforcement is a standard textbook term added for clarity; it is not on the slide. "Positive" and "negative" mean adding or removing, not good or bad.

    Managers should understand employees' personality and values to know what each person actually finds rewarding.

    5. Why reward systems fail, and how to fix them

    Signs of a poor reward system, quoted on the slide:

    It doesn't matter how hard I work around here. Who cares. Hey, we can get away with anything around here! If management doesn't care, why should I?

    Six elements of a recognition system

    1Commitment and support from top and middle management
    2Formal standards and expectations set in policy
    3A system of performance measurement
    4Consequences that participants see as meaningful
    5Fair and regular application
    6Periodic evaluation of the system

    A KPI is only one of six. If leaders do not back the system, rewards mean little to staff, or rules are applied unevenly, behavior will not change the way management wants.

    6. Schedules of reinforcement

    First ask: is the reward tied to time (interval) or to output (ratio)? Then: is the rule fixed or variable?

    ScheduleRuleEffect on behavior (slide)Slide example
    ContinuousEvery desired behaviorFast learning, fast extinctionCompliments
    Fixed intervalFixed timesAverage performance, rapid extinctionWeekly paychecks
    Variable intervalVariable timesModerate to high performance, slow extinctionPop quiz, unannounced inspection
    Fixed ratioFixed amount of outputHigh, stable performance, rapid extinctionPiece-rate pay
    Variable ratioVariable amount of outputVery high performance, slow extinctionCommissioned sales

    Extinction 消退 means a learned behavior fades when reinforcement stops. In a real case, classify by the actual reward rule: a fixed commission on every sale behaves more like fixed ratio.

    9. Common mistakes

    Task identity means the job is important.

    Identity is completing a whole job. Significance is impact on others.

    Autonomy tells employees how well they did.

    That is feedback. Autonomy creates responsibility.

    Happy workers always perform better.

    They may be related; the slide does not prove cause.

    Commitment and OCB are the same.

    Commitment is an attitude. OCB is voluntary behavior.

    Low morale means people will resign.

    They may also voice, wait or neglect.

    Negative reinforcement is punishment.

    It removes something unpleasant to increase a behavior.

    Any sales commission is variable ratio.

    Check the actual rule linking reward to output.

    High KPI means organizational success.

    The system may reward the wrong output.

    10. Cold call practice

    What is the Job Characteristics Model?

    It links five job dimensions to three psychological states and to outcomes such as motivation, quality, satisfaction, and lower absenteeism and turnover.

    Which dimensions create experienced meaningfulness?

    Skill variety, task identity and task significance together.

    Autonomy versus feedback?

    Autonomy creates experienced responsibility. Feedback gives knowledge of actual results.

    What is growth-need strength?

    How strongly a person wants challenge and growth. It explains why people respond differently to enriched jobs.

    What is organizational commitment?

    Accepting the organization's goals and values, willingness to work hard for it, and wanting to stay.

    What is OCB?

    Discretionary helpful behavior outside the formal reward system, done without expecting a direct reward.

    What is EVLN?

    Four responses to dissatisfaction: Exit (active, destructive), Voice (active, constructive), Loyalty (passive, constructive), Neglect (passive, destructive).

    What is reinforcement theory?

    Consequences shape future behavior. Rewarded behavior tends to be repeated; punished behavior tends to decrease.

    Why can a reward system fail?

    Employees see no reliable link between effort, performance and meaningful, fairly applied consequences.

    Name the six recognition elements.

    Management support, formal standards, performance measurement, meaningful consequences, fair and regular application, periodic evaluation.

    How do the schedules differ?

    Interval schedules depend on time, ratio schedules on output. Fixed is predictable, variable is not. Continuous rewards every occurrence.
