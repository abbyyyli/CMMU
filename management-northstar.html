    Northstar Client Solutions

    Delivery improves. Quality, cooperation and growth come under pressure. Read the facts, choose a useful framework, and build a case-specific answer.

    Fictional practice case. Figures and events belong to this exercise.

    1 · Read the case2 · Analyze S1–S53 · Try six questions

    1. Read the complete case

    Northstar is a business software implementation company in Bangkok with 24 employees. Staff organize client data, configure systems and complete system launches.

    Six months ago, Mira became operations manager. Better scheduling and a cleared backlog increased on-time completion from 72% to 94%. The owner praised this result, but other concerns have emerged.

    1Mira’s working style

    Mira enjoys bringing people together to discuss new services. She often develops her ideas while talking. She usually starts with future possibilities and leaves the detailed implementation procedures to her colleagues.

    When choosing between options, she asks for comparisons of costs, profits and test results. Even close to a system launch, she may change direction: “If there is a better option, why should we decide so early?”

    She says, “I know that I like thinking aloud and that my ideas change quickly.” When staff explain that frequent changes create rework, she replies, “You should be more flexible.”

    2Company promises and employee experience

    The company has always emphasized “autonomy, learning and reliable service.” Ana joined because she strongly values learning new skills and solving problems independently.

    Mira has suspended training and asked everyone to focus on closing more cases. Even small customer remedies require her approval.

    Ana says, “I still enjoy solving clients’ problems, but the way the company works now no longer reflects the company I identified with.” She still completes assigned work, but no longer volunteers to help new employees.

    3Job design and rewards

    Previously, one employee managed a client’s entire implementation. The work is now divided into small, repetitive steps, and each employee handles only one part. Staff rarely learn whether the client’s launch succeeds or how their own work affects the result.

    Each employee must close 30 cases per week. Mira set the target without discussing differences in case difficulty. Individual closure rankings are published daily, but there is little discussion of quality or improvement.

    Employees earn THB 500 for every ten completed cases. Bonuses are paid together at month end. Once earned, a bonus is not withdrawn even if a case is later reopened. Helping colleagues does not count toward the reward.

    Reopened cases have increased from four to eleven per hundred cases.

    4Different employee responses

    Ana suggests testing a new process that would maintain efficiency and reduce rework. Another employee resigns, saying that there are no opportunities for growth.

    Ben prefers clear, stable procedures and finds the new checklists helpful. He believes management will improve the situation, so he continues to support the company and waits.

    Another employee deliberately delays replies to clients and does only the minimum required.

    5A team meeting

    After Mira makes a last-minute change in priorities, an employee explains that two deadlines now conflict. She interrupts him in front of the team: “If you cannot even handle this, perhaps you are not right for this role.”

    Ana later suggests that Mira should first listen to the employee’s difficulties. Mira replies, “I am maintaining standards. I do not need you to tell me how to manage.”

    Afterward, employees raise fewer concerns during meetings. No one volunteers to lead the new improvement project.

    6The company’s direction

    Mira is good at presenting new ideas to clients and has improved delivery discipline. However, staff hear a new slogan every week and do not know what takes priority when speed, quality and development conflict.

    The owner asks Mira for a plan that preserves the gains in on-time delivery while improving quality, cooperation and retention.

    2. Facts first, then a useful lens 選框架

    A fact is reported in the case. An inference explains it through a concept. An assumption supplies a needed premise that the case does not state. Keep these separate.

    3. S1 · Personality and people management

    Mira’s scheduling has helped delivery. However, job design, rewards, people and culture also affect performance. Personality is one part of the explanation.

    Management elementCase evidence
    LeaderMira’s preferences, decisions, communication and response to criticism.
    PeopleAna values learning and autonomy; Ben prefers stable procedures.
    Process and cultureFragmented jobs, daily rankings, weak quality discussion and less employee voice.
    OutcomesFaster delivery, more reopened cases, less helping and a resignation linked to weak growth.

    A supported interpretation: ENTP 偏好

    Assume these repeated work habits reflect her usual preferences. Each letter needs its own evidence.

    E
    Extraversion

    She develops ideas through group discussion. Discussion is a strength; speaking before fully listening is a possible overuse.

    N
    Intuition

    She starts with future possibilities and leaves detailed procedures to others. New ideas help; implementation details can be missed.

    T
    Thinking

    She compares costs, profits and test results. Clear criteria help decisions; the effect on people still needs attention.

    P
    Perceiving

    She keeps options open and changes direction near launch. Flexibility helps, but late changes can create rework.

    Improved scheduling does not automatically make her J. Planning is a skill; keeping options open can remain a preference. T does not explain public threats: examine that conduct through EQ.

    Awareness of preferences

    She knows she thinks aloud and changes ideas quickly. The case does not say that she knows her full type.

    Awareness of impact

    She dismisses the rework caused by her changes. Understanding her impact on others is weaker.

    Ben’s stable-procedure preference is a J-like clue, not a full type. Ana’s wish for autonomy is clearer evidence of a value than an MBTI type.

    4. S2 · Values, attitudes and behavior

    Stated values versus daily practice 說做不一

    Autonomy

    Small customer remedies need Mira’s approval. Daily practice leaves little room for independent decisions.

    Learning

    Training stops while staff are told to close more cases.

    Reliable service

    The company promises reliable service, but its policy pays for closure counts even when cases later reopen. The reward policy does not fully support the stated value.

    The same fact can support three different arguments. The framework determines the question being asked.

    LensWhat this fact means
    ValuesReliable service is the stated value, but the reward policy gives little weight to lasting quality: a stated-practice discrepancy.
    Goal setting / MBOThirty closures per week measures quantity, not whether clients’ problems stay solved: weak alignment between the target and the intended result.
    ReinforcementTen closures earn THB 500 even if cases reopen: the consequence rewards closure volume more directly than lasting quality.

    Ana agrees with the stated values. The clearer problem is a stated-practice discrepancy: current practices do not support autonomy and learning. Mira’s actions give greater weight to speed, output and control.

    Ana values learning and independence. Ben prefers stable procedures. The same arrangement can help one employee and frustrate another.

    Separate Ana’s attitude from her behavior 態度與行為

    ComponentWhat the case supports
    CognitiveShe believes current practice no longer reflects the company she identified with.
    AffectiveShe still enjoys client problem-solving; her statement suggests disappointment with the organization.
    Behavioral intentionNo intention to resign is stated. Stopping voluntary help is an observed action, not a stated resignation intention.
    ConceptWhat changes, what remains
    Job satisfactionEnjoyment of client work remains. This does not mean satisfaction with every part of the job.
    Organizational commitmentIdentification with the company weakens, although she still offers improvements.
    OCBVoluntary help for newcomers declines.
    Task performanceAssigned work is still completed. Her individual quality results are not given.
    Job involvementEnjoying the work alone does not show how strongly the job forms part of her identity.

    中文邏輯知道偏好不等於知道影響；喜歡工作不等於認同公司；少做自願協助不等於本職績效下降。

    Daily rankings communicate “close more cases.” Public criticism suggests that raising concerns may be risky. These daily signals compete with the stated values. Mira shows some A-like impatience and performance pressure, but the case is not enough for a full A/B classification.

    5. S3 · Needs, management style and goals 動機

    The bonus is extrinsic. Ana’s enjoyment of solving client problems is intrinsic. External rewards do not directly replace learning, independence or meaningful work.

    Maslow: assess all five needs

    NeedEvidence → assessment
    PhysiologicalBonuses provide material rewards. Basic pay and living needs are not described enough to judge full support.
    SafetyMira publicly questions whether an employee fits the role. If staff interpret this as a job threat, insecurity is a possible gap.
    Social / belongingFragmented work, less helping and poor responses to concerns weaken cooperation and support.
    EsteemRankings recognize output, but public criticism harms respect. Quality and helping receive little recognition.
    Self-actualizationTraining stops and independent problem-solving is restricted. One employee leaves because growth is weak.

    ERG: several needs can matter at the same time

    ERG groups needs into Existence, Relatedness and Growth. A financial reward can support one group while the other groups remain weak.

    Need and meaningCase fact → assessment
    Existence: material basicsTHB 500 per ten cases provides a financial reward. This supports material needs, but base pay is not given, so full adequacy cannot be judged.
    Relatedness: supportive relationshipsMira rejects concerns, staff speak less and Ana stops helping newcomers. These facts show weaker support and cooperation, which are relatedness gaps.
    Growth: developing and using abilitiesTraining stops, jobs become small repeated steps and independent problem-solving is restricted. Staff have fewer chances to learn and use their abilities: a growth gap.

    ERG diagnosis: financial rewards coexist with relationship and growth gaps. These needs can matter together; receiving money does not remove the other needs.

    Frustration-regression requires a shift toward lower needs after growth is blocked. The case shows blocked growth, but not that shift.

    Herzberg: dissatisfaction and motivation are different

    Hygiene factors are conditions around the job; poor supervision or policy can create dissatisfaction. Motivators are growth, responsibility, achievement and recognition in the work itself.

    FactorCase fact → assessment
    Hygiene: supervision and policyMira publicly questions an employee’s suitability and dismisses Ana’s feedback. Even small remedies need approval. Poor treatment and restrictive policy can make the job frustrating.
    Hygiene: payA bonus is provided. This does not fix poor supervision, and the case gives no basis for judging base-pay adequacy.
    Motivators: growth and responsibilitySuspended training reduces learning. Fragmented jobs and approval rules reduce ownership and independent decisions. These weaken growth and responsibility.
    Motivators: achievement and recognitionRankings recognize case numbers, but staff rarely see successful client launches. Recognition is narrow and staff have less information about the value of their completed work.

    Herzberg diagnosis: poor treatment can create dissatisfaction, while restricted growth and ownership weaken motivation. A bonus alone does not solve either problem.

    ERG identifies which needs are weak. Herzberg explains why reducing dissatisfaction and building motivation require different conditions.

    Theory X/Y: belief → style → fit

    Theory X assumption

    Staff need external control and pressure because they avoid work or responsibility.

    Theory Y assumption

    Staff can accept responsibility and direct their own work when committed to clear goals.

    Reasoning stepCase-specific argument
    Observed management styleMira approves even small remedies, sets targets without staff input and rejects feedback. Decisions remain strongly controlled by her.
    Inferred assumptionAssume these rules reflect her belief that staff cannot make reliable decisions without close control. Under that assumption, the approach is X-like.
    Evidence of employee initiativeAna proposes a process to preserve efficiency and reduce rework. This shows a willingness to improve work and supports the Y view of potential responsibility.
    Management-fit diagnosisThe control-heavy style gives an employee who shows initiative little room to decide or contribute. This is a mismatch between the style and the initiative the company needs.

    Targets and bonuses alone do not identify Theory X. Ben’s preference for clear procedures also does not make him an “X employee”: clear structure can support Theory Y. MBTI describes Mira’s preferences; X/Y concerns her assumptions about employees.

    MBO: clear numbers, weak alignment

    ElementCase assessment
    SpecificityThirty closures is clear, but success focuses mainly on quantity.
    Time periodA weekly deadline is present.
    ParticipationMira sets the target without discussing case difficulty.
    FeedbackRankings show quantity. Quality, final outcomes and improvement feedback are weak.

    The main alignment gap is between rewarded closures and reliable service. Reopening does not change the earned reward. The rule creates a risk of premature closure or choosing easy cases, but those behaviors are not directly reported.

    6. S4 · Job design, responses and rewards

    JCM: the experience of the job

    DimensionCase fact → work experience
    Skill varietyRepeated small steps use fewer different skills.
    Task identityStaff handle one part rather than a complete implementation.
    Task significanceStaff cannot clearly see their effect on the client. This is unclear perceived impact, not proof the job is unimportant.
    AutonomySmall remedies require Mira’s approval.
    FeedbackClosure counts are visible, but successful client launches and quality outcomes rarely return to staff.
    Meaningfulness

    Variety + whole work + visible client impact

    Responsibility

    Freedom within clear decision limits

    Knowledge of results

    Feedback on actual client outcomes

    Ana values learning, so reduced development may affect her strongly. Ben’s liking for procedures does not show that he has low growth needs.

    EVLN: four different responses 員工反應

    Active · constructive
    Voice

    Ana proposes a better process.

    Active · destructive
    Exit

    An employee resigns because growth is weak.

    Passive · constructive
    Loyalty

    Ben supports the company and waits for improvement.

    Passive · destructive
    Neglect

    An employee delays replies and does the minimum.

    “Destructive” is a model category, not a moral judgment about a person who leaves. Silence is not automatically loyalty: after public criticism it may reflect fear or withdrawal.

    Fixed ratio: FR10. Monthly payment does not turn an output-based earning rule into fixed interval.

    Management supportSpeed has visible support; quality and cooperation receive less.
    StandardsVolume and bonus rules are clear; quality and cooperation standards are weaker.

    8. Bring the diagnosis together

    Mira improves delivery, but the company rewards quantity more clearly than quality and cooperation. Jobs offer less ownership, learning and independence. Her response to concerns also reduces voice. Together, these gaps explain why bonuses can exist while motivation, cooperation and retention weaken.

    9. Six questions: write first, then compare

    The questions do not name a required theory. Model answers show reasonable choices, not the only possible frameworks.

    All six model answers start closed.

    Q1 · Why are employees losing engagement despite increased bonuses?

    Choose the framework that best explains the problem. Identify which arrangements help, where the gaps are and how they affect employees. Do not simply list theory names.

    Show model answer

    Selected framework: Herzberg’s two-factor theory

    I would use Herzberg to separate dissatisfaction from motivation. Mira publicly questions an employee’s suitability and rejects feedback. These are supervision problems, so they concern hygiene factors and can create dissatisfaction even when a bonus is paid.

    Training stops, employees handle only small parts of a case and even minor decisions need approval. These facts show weaker growth and responsibility, which are motivators. Rankings recognize quantity, but employees rarely see successful client outcomes.

    The bonus may encourage closures, and checklists help Ben. However, money does not replace respectful supervision, learning, responsibility or meaningful achievement.

    The case gives the current bonus rule, but no before-and-after bonus amount. This answer explains why the current scheme is not enough.

    Q2 · What has changed for Ana, and what do the other employees’ responses mean?

    Analyze Ana’s statements and behavior. Separate what has changed from what remains. Then explain the different employee responses using suitable concepts.

    Show model answer

    Selected concepts: values, attitudes, OCB and EVLN

    Ana’s values fit the company’s stated values, but not its current practices. She values learning and autonomy, while training is stopped and small decisions need approval.

    She still enjoys helping clients and completes assigned work. Her identification with the company has weakened, and she no longer volunteers to help newcomers. This shows weaker organizational identification and lower OCB, rather than a complete loss of interest in her work. She has not stated an intention to resign.

    Her process suggestion is Voice. The employee who resigns shows Exit. Ben shows Loyalty because he supports the company and waits. Delaying client replies and doing the minimum are Neglect.

    Q3 · How would you interpret Mira’s working preferences?

    Propose a personality type and support each preference with case evidence. Explain her awareness of her own preferences and suggest a suitable way to communicate an improvement proposal. Do not simply guess four letters.

    Show model answer

    Selected framework: MBTI preferences

    Assuming these are her usual working preferences, I would propose ENTP. She develops ideas through discussion, supporting E. She focuses on future possibilities rather than detailed procedures, supporting N. She compares costs, profits and test results, supporting T. She keeps options open and changes direction late, supporting P.

    She knows she thinks aloud and changes ideas quickly. The case does not show that she knows her full type. She also dismisses the rework caused by her changes, so awareness of her impact is weak.

    I would discuss two improvement options with her, explain the wider benefits and compare costs and quality results. We could agree on one short trial and a clear review date.

    Q4 · Which two aspects of job design and rewards should change first?

    Choose suitable frameworks and explain how the arrangements create problems. For each change, explain what should change, why it may work and how the existing gains can be protected.

    Show model answer

    Selected frameworks: JCM, goal setting and reinforcement

    1 · Restore meaningful ownership

    I would give an employee or small team responsibility for a client case from the beginning to confirmed completion. They should receive final client feedback and have clear limits for routine decisions. This improves task identity, autonomy and feedback. Seeing client results also makes the work more meaningful.

    I would keep useful checklists and scheduling controls, and review resolution quality, rework and on-time delivery.

    2 · Reward reliable completion

    I would consider closure volume together with solution quality and team results. Staff should discuss case difficulty when targets are set. A reopened case should be reviewed to understand its cause; staff should not lose rewards simply for honestly reporting a problem.

    This reduces pressure to focus only on numbers. Track on-time delivery and reopened cases together, so improving one does not damage the other.

    Q5 · If you were Mira’s executive coach, how would you analyze and support her?

    Choose a suitable framework and examine all its dimensions. Connect each problem to a specific event. Propose matching practices or management tasks and a way to assess improvement. State any assumptions where facts are incomplete.

    Show model answer

    Selected framework: the five EQ factors

    I would use EQ because the case shows problems in self-understanding, responses to others and shared direction. Coaching should preserve Mira’s delivery strengths while changing the behaviors that harm the team.

    1 · Self-awareness and self-control

    She knows some habits but dismisses their impact and publicly threatens an employee. Review one real incident with the coach: her intention, her words and the staff experience. Before correction, pause and address factual problems privately.

    Check: Check later corrections for respect and usefulness; compare her account with confidential staff feedback.

    2 · Empathy

    She does not first understand conflicting deadlines. When a workload concern arises, listen, repeat the problem in her own words and agree priorities before adding work.

    Check: Check whether staff felt understood and whether the agreed priorities resolved the conflict.

    3 · Social expertise

    She rejects feedback and staff speak less. In the regular team review, invite concerns, let people finish and respond without personal attack. Agree an owner and follow-up date for each issue.

    Check: Check useful responses and follow-through, and whether staff become more willing to raise problems.

    4 · Personal influence

    No one volunteers for the project. Explain why it matters, invite staff ideas and give a willing project leader real support and clear decision rights. Recognize specific contributions.

    Check: Look for useful voluntary contributions and completed improvements, rather than praise counts alone.

    5 · Mastery of vision

    Staff hear changing slogans but lack priorities. Mira and the owner should agree on a clear direction and practical decision rules, then use them consistently when explaining goals and changes.

    Check: Ask staff to explain the priorities and apply them to a real deadline-versus-quality problem.

    Use the same regular team review where possible. Five factors do not require five separate meeting programs. The owner should review behavior, staff feedback and delivery results together.

    Q6 · You can recommend only three priority actions to the owner. What would you choose?

    Combine the analysis into one plan. Explain the problem each action addresses, how the actions work together and which of Mira’s strengths should be retained.

    Show model answer

    1 · Align goals and rewards

    Define success as timely and reliable completion. Consider case difficulty, and include quality and team results in rewards. This closes the gap between case numbers and reliable service.

    2 · Restore ownership and learning

    Give staff ownership of complete cases, clear limits for routine decisions and learning opportunities. Return final client results to the people doing the work. This supports autonomy, responsibility, growth and problem-solving.

    3 · Change the response to concerns

    Make respectful correction, listening and follow-through part of Mira’s responsibilities. The owner and coach review real incidents and staff feedback. This helps employees raise problems early and join improvements.

    The first action defines the right result. The second gives staff the ability and responsibility to achieve it. The third allows people to speak and contribute. Keep Mira’s scheduling discipline, useful checklists and ability to present ideas. Protect on-time delivery while reducing reopened cases and improving cooperation and retention.
