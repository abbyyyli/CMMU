    1. Culture and communication context

    In Hofstede's definition, culture is collective mental programming learned through shared education and life experience. Family, institutions and repeated experience turn expectations into habits. A new “speak up” policy can therefore arrive faster than employees' willingness to challenge a senior person.

    Use cultural patterns to identify expectations; use actual behavior to explain the person.

    High context 高情境
    Words+Relationship+Situation and tone

    Meaning depends heavily on shared context.

    Lecture examples: Thai smile and Wai.
    Low context 低情境
    Explicit words+Clear detail→Message

    More of the meaning is stated directly.

    Lecture example: USA.

    How a message gets misread

    Boss asks

    “Any objections?”

    →
    Employee responds

    A smile and silence.

    →
    Boss assumes

    “Everyone agrees.”

    The missing step is checking meaning. Ask for one concern or risk, and offer a private or written reply. Agreement, politeness and hesitation can look similar from the outside.

    中文邏輯先理解語氣與情境，再確認雙方對決定的共同理解。

    2. Five dimensions: definition and application

    The five main dimensions compare different cultural expectations. The lecture also introduces Indulgence/Restraint: how desires and impulses are regulated.

    DimensionDefinition and contrastWorkplace exampleRelated distinction
    Power Distance (PD) 權力距離Acceptance of differences in authority and status.
    Higher: greater deference to rank.
    Lower: smaller status differences.    		A junior employee chooses a private discussion to raise a concern with a senior manager.Status expectations concern rank; technical ability concerns expertise.
    Individualism (IDV) 個人／集體Individual versus group-centered identity and obligations.
    Higher: personal independence and goals.
    Lower: belonging, duties and “we.”    		Agree whether recognition should emphasize individual ownership, team success or both.IDV concerns individual/group obligations; I/E concerns personal processing preferences.
    Uncertainty Avoidance (UA) 不確定性規避Preference for structure over ambiguity.
    Higher: rules, expertise and predictability.
    Lower: more room for experimentation.    		Before a new process launches, staff ask for clear roles, written procedures and a review point.UA concerns ambiguity; J/P concerns how a person structures work.
    Masculinity (MAS) 成就／關懷Achievement versus care and quality of life.
    Higher: competition, money and assertiveness.
    Lower: harmony, care and life quality.    		A reward discussion balances performance targets with employee well-being and relationships.MAS concerns achievement/care values; EQ concerns emotional skills.
    Time Orientation (LTO) 時間取向Longer-run versus shorter-run focus.
    Higher: long-term orientation on the index.
    Lower: shorter-term priorities.    		Clarify how an immediate milestone contributes to a longer development plan.LTO concerns time horizons; planning turns goals into schedules and milestones.
    PD: how is rank accepted?

    Who may challenge whom, and how should disagreement be raised?

    UA: how is uncertainty handled?

    How much structure is needed before people can act?

    4. Conflict: different assumptions

    “Western” assumptions on the slide

    Productivity is central. Managed conflict, collaboration and confrontation can improve solutions; face-saving may weaken effectiveness.

    “Thai” assumptions on the slide

    Harmony, face and boss loyalty matter. Relationships and hierarchy may take priority; unspoken conflict can remain even when confrontation is avoided.

    Source: Roongrengsuke and Chansuthus (1998), cited on lecture pp.20–21.

    Invite concerns→Offer a suitable channel→Discuss evidence→Confirm the decision

    5. Time orientation and personality

    Lecture illustrationEmphasis
    Japan: longer termThrift, future savings, persistence and connections across time
    USA/Britain/Australia: shorter termTask schedules, urgency and getting things done
    Thailand: mixedQuick results alongside a longer view in Buddhist thinking

    Long- and short-term tendencies can coexist. The lecture links the longer view to patience, cycles and less emphasis on immediate goals. In practice, connect persistence toward a longer purpose with milestones for current work.

    Culture and personality operate at different levels. The lecturer suggests I could weaken presumed group orientation and J could strengthen performance emphasis in a Thai example. Use this as an interaction idea: ask how the individual prefers to process information, decide and work with others.

    7. Four-boss activity: background, gap and action

    The class considered how a Thai employee could work with Chinese, Russian, Swiss and INTJ Thai bosses. Read the cultural background, identify the difference in working expectations, then explain the adjustment.

    Source: S6 lecture activity and four group whiteboards, October 23, 2026. Country scores follow the lecturer’s comparison chart.

    Background→Gap→What to do
    Choose a boss

    Chinese boss

    Higher PD and achievement orientation combine with lower UA: respect authority, deliver results and adapt as work develops.

    Dimension and scoreMeaningGapWhat to do
    PD: 80 vs 64 (Δ +16)Higher PD emphasizes rank, titles and senior authority.A Thai employee is familiar with hierarchy; the boss may expect clearer deference and approval boundaries.Use respectful greetings and the preferred title.     IDV: 43 vs 19 (Δ +24)Higher relative IDV gives more weight to individual ideas and responsibility.The employee may wait for group consensus, while the boss expects a recommendation and a named owner.Share a reasoned proposal, own a deliverable and identify the support needed from teammates.     UA: 30 vs 64 (Δ −34)Lower UA favors flexibility, changing plans and action with incomplete information.The employee wants complete instructions; the boss may expect an initial attempt followed by adjustment.Act within delegated authority.     8. Do not mix these up

    ConceptsFirst lensSecond lens
    Country index / individual preferenceA country index describes shared cultural expectations.Individual preferences describe a person’s information processing and working style.
    Context / PDContext explains how words, relationships and situations convey meaning.PD explains acceptance of authority and status differences.
    PD / UAPD addresses who decides and how rank shapes interaction.UA addresses the amount of structure and predictability people prefer.
    IDV / IIDV compares individual and group-centered obligations.I emphasizes inward reflection and independent information processing.
    MAS / EQMAS compares achievement and competition with care and quality of life.EQ concerns recognizing emotions and managing behavior and relationships.
    LTO / planningLTO describes the time horizon emphasized in cultural values.Planning translates goals into decisions, schedules and milestones.
    Calm conduct / agreementCalm conduct expresses emotional restraint and can help preserve harmony.Agreement becomes clear when people state concerns and confirm the decision.

    9. Cold call practice

    What is culture, and why can it resist change?

    Collective mental programming shaped by shared learning and experience. Early habits and institutions reinforce expectations.

    High context versus low context?

    High context relies more on shared knowledge and situational cues; low context communicates more explicitly.

    Name the five core dimensions in lecture order.

    Power Distance, Individualism/Collectivism, Uncertainty Avoidance, Masculinity/Femininity and Time Orientation. Indulgence/Restraint is additional.

    What do Jai Yen, Jai Ron and Sanuk illustrate?

    Respectively, restraint and harmony; directness, visible emotion and results; enjoyment and relationships in work/life.

    Why can clear rules coexist with resistance to restrictions?

    Clear rules provide predictability; flexible procedures preserve room to act. Useful structure balances both needs.

    How do culture, personality and EQ complement one another?

    Culture describes shared expectations; personality describes individual preferences; EQ concerns emotional awareness and relationship skills.
