    1. Culture and communication context

    In Hofstede's definition, culture is collective mental programming learned through shared education and life experience. Family, institutions and repeated experience turn expectations into habits. A new “speak up” policy can therefore arrive faster than employees' willingness to challenge a senior person.

    Use cultural patterns to identify expectations; use actual behavior to explain the person.

    High context 高情境
    Words+Relationship+Situation and tone

    Meaning depends heavily on shared context.

    Lecture examples: Thai smile and Wai.
    Low context 低情境
    Explicit words+Clear detail→Message

    More of the meaning is stated directly.

    Lecture example: USA.

    How a message gets misread

    Boss asks

    “Any objections?”

    →
    Employee responds

    A smile and silence.

    →
    Boss assumes

    “Everyone agrees.”

    The missing step is checking meaning. Ask for one concern or risk, and offer a private or written reply. Agreement, politeness and hesitation can look similar from the outside.

    中文邏輯訊號不等於完整意思；先確認對方怎麼理解，再把決定說清楚。

    4. Conflict: different assumptions

    “Western” assumptions on the slide

    Productivity is central. Managed conflict, collaboration and confrontation can improve solutions; face-saving may weaken effectiveness.

    “Thai” assumptions on the slide

    Harmony, face and boss loyalty matter. Relationships and hierarchy may take priority; unspoken conflict can remain even when confrontation is avoided.

    Source: Roongrengsuke and Chansuthus (1998), cited on lecture pp.20–21.

    Invite concerns→Offer a suitable channel→Discuss evidence→Confirm the decision

    中文邏輯沒有公開衝突，不代表沒有問題；直接討論，也不等於可以羞辱對方。

    5. Time orientation and personality

    Lecture illustrationEmphasis
    Japan: longer termThrift, future savings, persistence and connections across time
    USA/Britain/Australia: shorter termTask schedules, urgency and getting things done
    Thailand: mixedQuick results alongside a longer view in Buddhist thinking

    Long- and short-term tendencies can coexist. The lecture links the longer view to patience, cycles and less emphasis on immediate goals. In practice, connect persistence toward a longer purpose with milestones for current work.

    Culture and personality operate at different levels. The lecturer suggests I could weaken presumed group orientation and J could strengthen performance emphasis in a Thai example. Use this as an interaction idea: ask how the individual prefers to process information, decide and work with others.

    7. Four-boss preparation activity

    Choose one boss and present how a hypothetical “typical Thai” employee should prepare: Chinese, Russian, Swiss or INTJ Thai. Use a relevant dimension, explain a possible mismatch, and ask about actual expectations.

    8. Do not mix these up

    PD 80 means 80% of managers are authoritarian.

    PD is a country-level index of accepted authority differences, not a percentage or a personal score.

    High context and high PD describe the same thing.

    Context asks where meaning comes from; PD asks how authority differences are accepted. Reading a smile and challenging a boss are different questions.

    High PD means high UA.

    PD concerns rank; UA concerns ambiguity. China and Russia both have high PD, but their UA scores are 30 and 95.

    Individualism means introversion or refusing teamwork.

    IDV contrasts individual and group obligations. E/I describes personal energy and processing preferences; individual ownership can coexist with teamwork.

    Masculinity measures biological sex or emotional skill.

    MAS contrasts achievement and assertiveness with care and quality of life. It is a cultural value orientation, not a gender or EQ test.

    Long-term orientation means better planning.

    LTO compares time horizons. A short-term deadline is a task requirement; it does not measure a worker’s cultural orientation.

    A calm employee agrees and is satisfied.

    Calm conduct can coexist with unspoken conflict. Check the employee’s meaning, concerns and work attitudes before treating silence as agreement.

    INTJ identifies a manager’s Hofstede scores.

    INTJ describes individual preferences; country indices describe shared patterns. Use both to frame questions, then check the person’s actual expectations.

    9. Cold call practice

    What is culture, and why can it resist change?

    Collective mental programming shaped by shared learning and experience. Early habits and institutions reinforce expectations.

    High context versus low context?

    High context relies more on shared knowledge and situational cues; low context communicates more explicitly. Neither is inherently better.

    Name the five core dimensions in lecture order.

    Power Distance, Individualism/Collectivism, Uncertainty Avoidance, Masculinity/Femininity and Time Orientation. Indulgence/Restraint is additional.

    What do Jai Yen, Jai Ron and Sanuk illustrate?

    Respectively, restraint and harmony; directness, visible emotion and results; enjoyment and relationships in work/life.

    Why can clear rules coexist with resistance to restrictions?

    Predictability can be helpful while excessive controls still limit freedom. UA does not imply approval of every rule.

    Can INTJ establish cultural scores or EQ?

    No. Personality preferences, country indices and emotional skills are different constructs.
