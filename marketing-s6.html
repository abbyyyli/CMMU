    2. Perceptual map: a complete worked example

    Key idea

    Choose a category and buying occasion. Plot one focal brand, one direct rival and one indirect rival. Explain the position with two reasons.

    RoleChoiceWhy it belongs
    Focal brandGatoradeCategory: sports drinks; occasion: hydration after exercise.
    Direct competitor 直接競爭Pocari SweatA similar-category drink considered for the same occasion.
    Indirect competitor 間接競爭Plain bottled waterA different category that can satisfy the hydration need.

    How it works

    Use sports-performance image and everyday-hydration suitability as the axes. These compare what the brands mean to buyers.

    Interactive · Perceptual map

    Select a brand. Move its point with the sliders, drag it, or focus the point and use the arrow keys.

    Worked answer

    I would position Gatorade around hydration after exercise. Pocari Sweat is a direct sports-drink rival. Plain water is an indirect way to meet the hydration need.

    Reason 1: clear recall. A link with exercise gives buyers a reason to remember the brand when that occasion comes.

    Reason 2: a useful choice. A clear exercise-related benefit gives people a reason to choose it over basic water. Keep that promise clear against Pocari Sweat and support it with the product and message.

    How it works

    Drawing order: category and occasion → required rivals → two axes with low/high labels → three plotted names → position and two reasons. S5 uses one direct + one indirect.

    3. Research choice: answer the information need

    Need to knowMethodWhy it fits / limit
    Why people visit or stop visitingQualitative depth interviews or focus groups.Open questions reveal motives and occasions. A few interviews cannot estimate the percentage of the whole market.
    Age, gender, residence, mall choice and visit frequencyQuantitative structured survey; behavioral records when available.Comparable answers measure segment sizes and patterns. Recruit across relevant locations and times.
    Feelings about online versus mall shoppingInterviews/focus groups first; survey defined attitudes later if needed.Explore convenience, trust, product inspection and social enjoyment. A survey can then measure how widespread the themes are.
    What shoppers actually doObservation or transaction data.Behavior is visible, but the reason behind it is not directly observed.

    中文邏輯Q4 的 a、b、c 要全部回答；不是三選一。每項至少寫「方法＋為何能回答這個目的」。

    Example

    A cleaner can remove stains and smell pleasant. Functional and enjoyment benefits can work together. Ask about both when studying customer motives.

    4. Compare countries with three different reasons

    Key idea

    Choose a country. Compare it directly with Thailand on three different points. Each point must explain a business effect, such as demand, competition or cost.

    ComparisonWhat to compare on both sidesBusiness implication
    Customer demandPurchasing power and shopping occasions in reachable target cities.A retailer needs buyers who can and want to buy its actual basket, not population alone.
    Competitive accessExisting stores, underserved catchments and customer substitutes.Expansion should reach additional demand rather than mainly transfer sales between nearby stores.
    Company feasibilityLocal capability, sites, logistics, investment and expected contribution.An attractive market still fails if the format cannot be delivered profitably.

    How it works

    SWOT separates company strengths and weaknesses from outside opportunities and threats. Use the factors to choose an action.

    5. Growth: identify what is actually additional

    Key idea

    Look for what the whole business gains. Online orders can replace store orders. A bundle can bring next month’s purchase forward. Count added purchases and contribution after these effects and costs.

    ActionHow it could grow salesCheck
    New usersReach an underserved segment or catchment.Additional customers and repeat purchases, not reach alone.
    New usesCreate another relevant occasion for the same product.Genuine additional occasions, not switching from another company product.
    Higher usage / multipacksMore availability can increase consumption.Separate consumption from stockpiling, earlier buying and waste.
    Direct selling / disintermediation 去中介化Bypass a reseller and control the customer relationship.Fulfillment, returns and partner conflict can offset saved intermediary margin.
    DownsizingKeep the shelf price while reducing quantity.330 → 320 mL: quantity −3.03%; price/mL +3.125%, if price is unchanged. Check disclosure and trust.

    How it works

    For a channel question, state the choice, two reasons, two extra ideas and the result to measure. Use S5 for package-size calculations.

    6. Customer value, targeting and relationships

    Key idea

    STP (Segmentation, Targeting, and Positioning) means grouping customers by relevant needs, choosing whom to serve and building a clear meaning for the brand.

    ConceptMeaning and exampleDo not confuse with
    Needs, wants and occasionsA need for food can lead to different meals depending on time, companions and mood.The same person does not want the same offer in every situation.
    Satisfaction versus repeat choiceDiminishing marginal utility and variety-seeking can lead a satisfied customer to choose something else.Switching is not always dissatisfaction.
    Benefit, USP (Unique Selling Proposition) and RTB (Reason to Believe) 可信理由Benefit: why it helps. USP: distinct selling claim. RTB: support for believing the claim.A copied feature or scientific-sounding ingredient does not establish customer value.
    Buyer versus userA donor buys medical supplies for monks; the chooser and end user have different roles.Researching users alone can miss the purchase decision.
    Demographic versus psychographicAge/income describe people; motives, values and lifestyles help explain choices.Age does not determine classical-music preference or a universal generation mindset.
    CRM (Customer Relationship Management) and customer experienceManage the relationship through actual touchpoints, service and relevant communication.A loyalty reward is one tactic; enrollment alone is not retention.

    Example

    Greyhound’s birthday benefit was blocked because the professor’s membership had expired. The earlier VIP (Very Important Person) relationship did not carry through to that visit. Improve reminders and service recovery, then track return visits and contribution.

    10. Cold call practice

    What is positioning?

    The meaning a brand is intended to hold in a chosen customer’s mind relative to alternatives. It needs a clear target, a valued benefit and consistent delivery.

    Why can a satisfied customer switch?

    The occasion or desired benefit can change, and variety-seeking can occur without dissatisfaction. Investigate the reason before offering a discount.

    How do three country reasons differ?

    They explain different mechanisms, such as reachable demand, competitive access and operating feasibility. Rephrasing population size is still one reason.

    When can a survey measure attitudes?

    When the attitude and response scale are clearly defined, a suitable survey can estimate prevalence or strength. Interviews can first discover the themes.

    What distinguishes extra online revenue from extra company profit?

    Online purchases may replace store purchases and bring fulfillment, return or acquisition costs. Assess incremental contribution after those effects.

    11. Answer templates: short, complete reasoning

    Key idea

    State the decision. Use a relevant case fact. Explain why it matters. Add the next distinct reason and the main trade-off. A metric supports this answer; it does not replace it.

    Worked answer

    Value claim: I would not rely on a special water ingredient alone. The feature needs a credible benefit that customers value enough to change purchase or repeat behavior. Test the promised outcome and customer response before charging a premium.

    CRM decision: I would contact recently lapsed customers with a relevant reason to return. A reminder addresses missed communication; an improved visit addresses the experience. Compare repeat visits and contribution with a similar untreated group to judge whether the action added value.
