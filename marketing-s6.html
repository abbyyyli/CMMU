    2. Changing needs, meaningful value and the funnel

    A restaurant customer needs food, but “food” does not specify the desired meal. Hunger, companions, time, mood and location change the benefit sought. A favorite dish may offer less additional satisfaction when eaten repeatedly: diminishing marginal utility 邊際效用遞減. Switching for novelty is variety-seeking; it can occur without dissatisfaction. The same person can therefore choose a different brand next week while still liking the first.

    Different occasion→Different valued benefit→Different choice

    A USP is a distinct selling claim; an RTB 可信理由 supports belief in a promised benefit. Shampoo features may use similar ingredients or be copied. A useful difference must be noticed, valued, believed and delivered at a viable cost. The lecturer’s skepticism does not establish that all shampoos perform identically.

    4Ps + PeopleProduct, Price, Place and Promotion must reinforce the promise; service staff help deliver it. Seasonality and payday are context variables, not replacement Ps.Availability, coherent experience and customer response.
    FunnelAwareness → interest → trial → repeat; each transition can fail.Measure the stage matching the objective; views alone do not prove purchase or profit.

    3. Relationships, local fit and brand identity

    In the Greyhound anecdote, a former heavy user had received VIP attention, but an expired membership card later blocked a birthday ice-cream reward. The disappointment connects CRM with CEM/CXM: managing an ongoing relationship and the experience of real touchpoints. A reward rule can be applied correctly while the experience fails to recognize relationship history.

    A sensible diagnosis checks expiration reminders, falling frequency, staff recovery options and the cost of intervention. The anecdote cannot establish why the reminder failed, what usable data existed, or whether a reward would have retained the customer. Enrollment and discounts remain mechanisms; repeat behavior and incremental contribution are outcomes.

    4. Buyer, user and the limits of narrow targeting

    Medical supplies for Buddhist monks reveal a buyer–user distinction 買家與使用者: a monk may use the product while a lay donor chooses and pays. Researching only the end user can miss the buying decision. A niche can improve relevance and willingness to pay, yet fewer reachable buyers and fewer purchase occasions can cap growth. Broad positioning can expand occasions but still needs a coherent benefit.

    ExampleBusiness mechanismWhat remains unproven
    Brand’s Essence of ChickenMessages for students, hospital patients and older people illustrate several need states under one brand.Education/health outcomes, nutrition comparisons and profitability are not established by the anecdote.
    Phillip Wain women-only gymA narrow audience may value the setting while excluding other buyers.The lecturer’s opinion does not establish that women-only gyms underperform.
    Dom PérignonPrestige and celebration occasions can support a premium while limiting frequency.Historical price anecdotes do not establish current prices or total demand.

    Demographics describe age, income or life stage; psychographics explore interests, values and habits. Classical-music preferences cannot be deduced from age alone. Cohort labels also need local evidence: the recap’s reference to formative experiences around ages 17–25 is the lecturer’s heuristic, not a universal boundary. A narrowly described group is useful only if it changes a decision about value, access or communication.

    6. A scientific-sounding claim still needs a value chain

    Feature→Credible benefit→Benefit survives until use→Customer values it→Choice + viable margin

    Iceberg-water beer illustrates the missing link between an unusual input and willingness to pay. Special sourcing may add cost or supply risk without improving a benefit the buyer notices. The same test applies to oxygen-enhanced, O3-labeled or “activated” water: a technical description does not establish a health outcome. The recap supplies anecdotes and claims, not independent scientific verification.

    7. Match research to the uncertainty; compare markets fairly

    Functional and experiential motives can coexist: detergent may remove stains and provide a valued fragrance. Start with what must be learned. Interviews explore reasons; measurement estimates their prevalence; observation records actions; a credible intervention tests whether an action changes behavior. One method does not answer every question.

    Information neededStarting methodLimit
    Why people stopped visitingDepth interviews with relevant former visitors; examine records first.Reported reasons do not estimate the whole market or prove a cause.
    Who visits, how often and how muchStructured survey with suitable sampling; behavioral records where available.A large sample alone is not representative.
    Feelings about online versus mallsQualitative exploration; quantify defined attitudes if prevalence matters.Stated attitudes are not actual purchases.

    For a country comparison, use the same unit of analysis on both sides. Compare target-city purchasing power, unmet shopping occasions, competitive alternatives, distribution and operating costs. A national population advantage may disappear when only reachable buyers can afford the retail basket. Religion or cultural acceptance may also constrain a particular product. Convert each fact into a mechanism and compare it directly with Thailand.

    9. Real substitutes, channel costs and ethical growth

    A shopper may replace a fruit drink with water, a sports drink or another fruit drink on the same occasion. Direct/indirect labels depend on the category boundary; managerial competition concerns actual alternatives. Public or gift-giving choices may also value presentation and status more than private routine consumption. Neither a category label nor a public image predicts every person’s choice.

    Growth movePotential valueRequired check
    Direct selling / disintermediationMore customer access, control or retained margin.Acquisition, fulfillment, returns and retailer conflict can offset the gain.
    New users / new usesMore buyers or additional valuable occasions.Reach, repeat frequency, genuine need and internal substitution.
    More usage / multipacksAvailability at home may increase consumption.Separate extra consumption from stockpiling, earlier purchases and wasted quantity.
    DownsizingLess content can preserve the shelf price while raising price per unit.Net quantity, disclosure, repeat behavior, margin and trust.

    For the recap’s 330 → 320 mL comparison at an unchanged price: quantity falls by 10/330 = 3.03%, while unit price rises by 330/320 − 1 = 3.125%. Package appearance does not determine net content. The observations concern historical examples; the arithmetic depends on unchanged price. The S5 calculator explores other sizes.

    11. Cold call practice

    Why can a satisfied customer switch?

    The occasion, available alternatives or desire for variety may change. Satisfaction is an evaluation; repeat choice is behavior under a particular context.

    Why is buyer different from user?

    The person choosing or paying may seek benefits different from those of the end user, as with a donor buying supplies for monks. Both roles can matter to research and communication.

    What makes two reasons distinct?

    Each explains a different mechanism, such as customer access versus operating cost. Restating “large market” as “many people” does not create a second reason.

    When can attitudes be measured quantitatively?

    After the attitude or construct has been defined, a suitable survey can measure its prevalence or intensity. Exploratory interviews can first uncover meanings that fixed options might miss.

    Why is a BCG progression not compulsory?

    Its categories depend on market growth and relative share, which can change differently. PLC describes sales over time and is a different tool.

    What is the final business test?

    Whether the action produces valued customer behavior and incremental contribution after relevant costs, while preserving truthful communication and trust.
